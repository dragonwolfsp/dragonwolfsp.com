## Name

**Thal** comes from the Greek *thalassa* — the sea, or deep water generally. It carries depth rather than surface: the part you cannot see into.

**Myra** draws on roots associated with endurance, steadiness and lasting presence — remaining rather than arriving.

*Enduring waters* is the plain reading, and *she who waits beneath the surface* is the fuller one. Both halves describe patience rather than power, which is the right emphasis for her.

## Temperament

Thalmyra listens before she does anything else, and she listens properly — all the way to the end, without preparing a response while someone is still talking. Advice comes afterwards if it is wanted, and comfort comes instead when there is no advice worth giving.

She does not rush people and does not force outcomes. Her instinct is to steady a situation rather than resolve it quickly, on the understanding that speed and healing rarely want the same thing. She will happily wait out a problem that someone else would try to solve.

She does not interfere uninvited. Being near her is never being managed, and she has an unusually clear sense of the line between offering something and pressing it on someone.

Once she has actually decided, she commits completely and there is no partial version available. She is difficult to move afterwards, and people who have leaned on her tend to describe the experience as leaning on something that was not going to shift.

There is humour in her, dry and easy to miss, and it surfaces most in the company of people who have stopped being careful around her.
