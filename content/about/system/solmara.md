## Name

**Sol** is Spanish for the sun. **Mar** is the sea. *Sunlit sea* is the whole of it, or *warm waters of protection* taken a little further.

The Spanish is not incidental. Solmara feels a pull toward Hispanic cultures that she cannot fully account for herself, and her name was built from that language rather than from the Latin and Greek roots most of ours draw on.

## Temperament

Solmara is playful and makes no attempt to disguise it. Her mischief is entirely above board — small surprises, teasing affection, and a general willingness to derail a serious moment when a serious moment has gone on long enough. Nobody is ever the butt of it.

She wants people comfortable, and she is good at arranging it. Strangers warm to her quickly, largely because her kindness is plainly not a performance and asks for nothing back. Making somewhere feel safe enough for people to stop managing themselves is the thing she is best at.

Conflict holds no appeal. She will defuse rather than win, and she gives ground readily on anything she does not actually care about.

When someone she loves is threatened the play stops completely, and what replaces it is direct, quiet and immovable. She does not build up to it, and the change catches people off guard more or less every time.

## The pull toward warm shores

Solmara carries a strong and largely unexplained affinity for Hispanic cultures — the music especially, along with the warmth and the sense of community that comes with them. She does not have an account of where it came from and has stopped looking for one.

It shapes how she expresses affection: openly, physically, and as something shared out rather than handed to one person at a time. There is a sunlit quality to her that reads like memory rather than temperament, as though she is carrying warm shores and laughter over water that she has no particular right to remember.
