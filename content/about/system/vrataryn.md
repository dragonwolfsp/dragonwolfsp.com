## Name

**Vrata** is Sanskrit, and it is larger than *vow* makes it sound. A vrata is the rule of conduct a person binds themselves to and then lives inside — an observance held to continuously, day after day, rather than a single promise made once and remembered afterwards.

**Aryn** carries guardian and protector, the same element that closes Solaryn.

*He who lives inside the rule* is the reading. He keeps a code, he will not break it, and the two halves of the name are the two halves of that: the discipline, and the people it exists for.

## Temperament

Vrataryn is a warrior, and he fights so that other people do not have to. He treats those as one sentence rather than two.

He lives by a code. It has a great many rules in it, he keeps all of them, and he will talk through any part of it with anyone who asks. Where it came from is the one thing he does not go into.

He knows he was built as a weapon and he has kept it. What he changed is who it points at — he uses what he is to protect rather than to destroy, and he sees nothing to resolve in that. He carries no affection at all for the people who made him, on the grounds that they misunderstood the thing they were building: a warrior fights on behalf of those who cannot, and they made him to conquer, which is a different job entirely and a smaller one.

He is good at it and he knows he is. The pride he takes in that is practical rather than vain, since better means the people in his care are safer. He enjoys fighting the way an athlete enjoys their sport — for the doing of it, for being good at it, and for still getting better — and the enjoyment sits comfortably alongside the fact that it is work worth doing.

He listens properly, and what comes back is considered rather than quick. He likes company and is entirely content without it, and neither state is him waiting for the other.

The closeness he wants is a small number of people who are close to one another as well as to him, and who are comfortable being near him physically — which is a different thing from requiring that they be. With someone new he is careful without being closed, and in a crowd he goes quieter and feels the connection thin out.

Nothing about him is hidden. He protects because he believes it is right, and because he considers it what he owes: his creators took something out of the world, he does not hold himself responsible for that, and he intends to put back more than was removed. Among people he trusts he is most fully himself, and with strangers he conceals very little — there are stories he will not tell and lines he does not let people over, and those are boundaries rather than secrets.

What turns him cold is any threat to a life that has done nothing to earn it. He has a particular contempt for people who go after those least able to answer back, and he does not distinguish between doing it with force and doing it with words.
