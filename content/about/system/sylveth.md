## Name

**Syl** draws on silk, weaving, and the quiet interconnectedness of threads that only become strong once they are joined to others.

**Veth** draws on roots tied to paths, journeys and enduring passage — the long kind, measured in seasons rather than days.

Together the name reads as *walker of the silken paths*, or a little more fully as keeper of the journey between endings and beginnings. Both halves are about connection over distance: silk holds things together, and a path is what makes two places one journey.

## Temperament

Sylveth is unhurried in a way that has nothing to do with reluctance. He watches first, at length, and it comes from a settled belief that a thing should be understood before it is touched — pull one thread without seeing the pattern and you find out afterwards what else was attached to it.

His comfort arrives as presence rather than speech. He tends to arrive quietly, sit down, and stay, and he is entirely content to say nothing for a long while. People who are struggling often find they have been sitting with him for an hour without having explained themselves once.

He is a predator and does not pretend otherwise. What he does with it is restraint — exercised so consistently that most people forget the capability is there at all, which he considers the correct outcome.

Darkness is restful to him rather than threatening. He treats endings the same way: not as the failure of something but as the far end of it, already tied to whatever comes next.

He notices the connections between people, decisions and consequences that others miss entirely, and he rarely says so unless it matters.
