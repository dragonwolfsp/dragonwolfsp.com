## Name

**Ani** comes from the Latin *anima* — life, soul, breath. All three at once, in the way the word originally worked: the animating thing, without a line drawn between being alive and being someone.

**Vara** draws on roots for guardianship and protection, running back through Old Norse *vörðr*, a watchman or warden.

*Living guardian* is the direct reading, and *the soul that protects* is the fuller one. Both halves are about vitality rather than defence — what she guards with is her aliveness, not a shield.

## Temperament

Anivara is usually the first to engage. New people, new situations, anything unfamiliar — she goes toward it while everyone else is still deciding, and she does it without much apparent risk assessment.

Her playfulness does real work. It loosens situations that were about to set, gets people talking who had decided not to, and defuses things that would otherwise have hardened into positions. It looks like frivolity and functions as strategy, though she would not describe it that way and probably has not thought about it.

She refuses stagnation, in herself and around her. Something that has stopped moving bothers her on an instinctive level, and she will poke at it until it starts again.

Injustice provokes her fast, particularly when it lands on someone vulnerable or on someone's happiness. Her protectiveness arrives at speed and is considerably fiercer than her ordinary manner suggests — warmth and sharp teeth are not opposites in her, and people who assumed otherwise tend to get the point quickly.

She is emotionally honest almost to a fault. What she feels is on the surface, she does not manage it for anyone's comfort, and she has very little patience for those who do.
