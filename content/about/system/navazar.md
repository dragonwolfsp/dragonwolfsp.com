> **Content note.** This page describes Navazar's view of intimacy, including sexual intimacy, and a blight that can control, hurt, kill or give pleasure to those it infects. They are confined to the Temperament section's last paragraph and to His blight, which are the parts to skip.

## Name

**Navāz** comes from the Persian نواز, from *navākhtan*, to caress, to cherish, to soothe.

**Zar** comes from the Persian زر, gold.

*The golden caress* is the reading. It holds his touch, the way he shows his care, and the gold of his eyes.

## Temperament

Navazar is open and honest, and he values both highly, in himself and in others. What he says is what he means.

His manner is gentle. He is often quiet, and he can be loud as well, because he follows the energy in the room and moves with it.

He protects the people around him in body and in mind alike, and he is both a dancer and a fighter.

Touch is how he shows affection, and how he helps people find their ground again. The moment anyone shows him they want him less close, or not touching them, he backs off, every time. To him intimacy is something beautiful, shared between people who trust each other, sexual or otherwise, and he will not let it be tainted.

## His blight

Navazar carries a power that is his alone, a blight. It was born when wild magic twisted dark magic together, and he wields what that twisting made. He holds no dark magic of his own.

The blight spreads through his touch, his stingers, his bite and his mandibles. If he allows it, it passes onward from one infected being to the next. He can also reach out with it at a short distance, though only physical contact makes certain it takes hold. It infects only as far as he wants it to, and he chooses how damaging or how lasting it is.

In the infected it shows as dark veins and markings, and as patches of dark brown chitin. It can grow a shell of chitin that covers them completely, and that shell is different on every being it grows over.

Through the blight he can take complete control of everyone infected, from any distance, and move them as one mind. He shares as much of their senses and their thoughts as he chooses. Beings that cannot move, such as plants, become extensions of the blight if he wishes, sensory organs for the whole and sources of energy he can draw on.

He can draw energy from the infected, enough to kill if he wanted to. He can also give it to them, from himself, or pass it between them.

The infected become far stronger, faster and harder to kill than they were. The blight heightens everything they already have, physical and magical alike.

Being infected can be extremely painful or deeply pleasurable. The blight answers his wishes completely. He can remove it whenever he chooses, and it leaves nothing behind.

It can heal as well. It drives every other disease out of a living host, and it mends wounds.

It has very few limits. He can hold as many beings as he likes without any drain on himself. Only the act of infecting draws on his strength, and he is strong enough that it takes a great deal to tire him. The blight reaches beings with no physical body, such as voids and shades, because it is magical. For the same reason, magic can fight it.
