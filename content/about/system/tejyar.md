## Name

**Tej** comes from the Sanskrit *tejas* — fiery energy, brilliance, vigour. It names the heat that lives in a person rather than the heat in a hearth: spirit, temper, the flare that comes up in someone when they are roused. That makes it the right fire for him, because it is his own rather than anything he calls on.

**Yar** comes from the Persian *yār* — friend, companion and beloved, all held in one word. It is the element in Shahryār, and it reaches further than the English *friend* does. A *yār* is the one who stays.

*Fiery friend* is the plain reading. The heat and the loyalty are the same thing in him, which is why the name sets them side by side.

## Temperament

Tejyar arrives talking. He teases inside the first minute and he aims it — the emotion affinity hands him the state of a room before he has crossed it, and what he does with that is pick out whoever most needs taking down a peg or drawing out. Very little of it costs anyone anything, whatever it sounds like on the way past. People who have been on the receiving end describe the same thing: being laughed at by Tejyar feels a great deal like being let in.

He is feisty, and it comes up fast. He argues, he flares, he takes a position and holds it well past the point where holding it is convenient. The heat drops as quickly as it rose, and he is generally the first one back afterwards — sideways, with a joke instead of an apology, which everyone who knows him has learned to accept as the apology.

His affection travels in disguise. He will insult somebody he loves with real invention and then rearrange his entire day around them, and the insult and the rearranging are one gesture. Ask him directly whether he cares and you get a flat denial delivered with his antennae standing straight up, which he is aware of, and which has never once stopped him trying it.

The loyalty is where the lightness stops. He is loyal to a fault and the fault is a real one: he will back somebody he loves past the point of good sense, put himself between them and things he has no business standing in front of, and go on doing it after being told to stop. He knows this about himself. He has shown no interest whatsoever in fixing it.

The slime does the practical work. He heals with it — a hand laid over a wound, the slime running firm and cool, and a running commentary the whole time about how the injury was avoidable, which it usually was. The commentary is how he carries being frightened for somebody, and nobody has ever mistaken it for anything else.

His thought-contact opens to the people closest to him, and once it is open it stays open. It arrives warm and loud and entirely his, turning up at odd hours with something that could easily have waited until morning. Being on that list is the most direct thing he does. He would never put it that way, and he has never needed to — everyone on it worked it out a long time ago.
