## Name

**Kae** comes from the Japanese 開 (*kai*, read *hiraku* as a verb) — to open. It covers opening a door, opening a book, and opening a road that was not there before, and the last of those is the sense carried here.

**Vor** is a Germanic and Norse-flavoured element tied to journeying, forward movement and the path itself rather than the destination at the end of it.

**-in** closes the name as an ordinary masculine ending.

Together it reads as *one who opens new paths* — someone for whom the going is the point, and for whom an unexplored direction is reason enough on its own.

## Temperament

Kaevorin is quiet on first meeting and it misleads almost everyone. He watches, listens, and takes a while to say anything, but none of it is shyness or caution — he is simply in no hurry, and he would rather understand the shape of a conversation before adding to it.

Once he is comfortable, that reverses entirely. An idea that catches him will carry him off completely: he talks quickly, gestures, follows tangents, and wants to know what happens if you push the thing one step further. Enthusiasm is his natural state and the reserve is just the door in front of it.

Curiosity is the thread through all of it. He is far more interested in a good question than a settled answer, and he treats being wrong as a useful outcome rather than an embarrassing one. New places, unfamiliar ideas and things he has no framework for are all straightforwardly appealing to him.

He is generous about bringing people with him. Discovery is better shared, in his view, and he has a habit of making unfamiliar things sound like invitations rather than tests — which tends to get people to try things they would otherwise have declined.

He is comfortable in his own variability in a way that some find unsettling. Presenting differently from one day to the next is not indecision for him, and he does not experience it as becoming anything other than himself.
