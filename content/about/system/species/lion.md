## Overview

Lions are the cooperative cat. Where the other big cats hunt alone, lions hunt together, and they live in prides built around related females who stay together across generations.

Every lion carries a tie to the sun.

## Physical traits

Powerfully built and comparatively compact, with a deep chest, muscular forequarters and retractable claws. Uniquely among cats, the tail ends in a dark tuft.

The mane is not a male trait here. Any lion may carry one — males tend toward the thicker, fuller mane, and females toward a smaller, fluffier one, but the mane itself is not restricted by sex. Fullness and darkness still carry information about condition, and both rival lions and potential mates read them.

The roar is produced by a specialised larynx with unfused hyoid bones and square vocal folds, letting a lion roar at volume with relatively little lung pressure. It carries roughly eight kilometres.

The sun-kissed tend to run larger than other lions.

## Prides

The pride structure carries over from the animal: related females at the core, with a coalition of males alongside them.

What differs is how leadership works. Leaders are chosen rather than dominant, and once chosen they typically hold the position for the rest of their lives — unless the pride decides they should not, which is the only thing that ends it.

## Magic

Every lion is tied to the sun. That is the one thing the species fixes.

The **sun-kissed** hold the strongest form of that connection and are recorded as a breed of their own. They are also typically larger than other lions.

Everything else is individual.

## Shared instincts

Lions hunt cooperatively, using positioned flanking rather than raw speed — they are not fast by big-cat standards and rely on coordination and cover. That cooperation is genuine and it is the species' most distinctive instinct.

Instincts are not temperament. A lion can act against any of them, and how any individual carries themselves is entirely their own.

## Lineage

Lions interbreed with tigers, as the animals do — ligers and tigons exist here for the same reasons they exist in the world.

They can also interbreed with [feliry](../feliry/), whose biology is close enough to make it possible.

## Where the name comes from

The lion (*Panthera leo*) ranges across sub-Saharan Africa, with a remnant population in the Gir Forest of India. Males run 150–250 kg and females 120–180 kg. Lionesses do the majority of the hunting in the wild, and males contribute more to defending territory against rival coalitions, which is genuinely dangerous work.

"King of beasts" is a European heraldic import rather than anything the animal supports, and it is not a reading we carry.

## Breeds

The sun-kissed are the one lion breed that is not simply a natural subspecies.
