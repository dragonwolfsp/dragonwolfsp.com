> **Content note.** This entry describes nemalor sexual anatomy, a growing process for the armour that involves the sexual organs and can be pleasurable, and an energy that can hurt or kill alongside one that can arouse. The filaments' energy, Growing the armour, and Intimacy and young are the sections to skip.

## Overview

Nemalor are a tall, slender, humanoid people. Fine, living filaments cover them from head to foot, and those filaments carry two energies: one that can paralyse, hurt or kill, and one that soothes.

They have a homeworld, and they are spacefaring, living among the stars aboard ships. How often a nemalor goes home is entirely up to them, with one exception: the armour. Nearly every nemalor has a set, a living suit grown around them in the magic caves of the homeworld, and that is the one journey home nearly every nemalor makes.

A nemalor lives for several centuries.

## Physical traits

A grown nemalor usually stands between eight and eleven feet tall, with a slender build.

Their skin is smooth beneath the filaments, and ranges from deep blues and dark violets to cool blue-greys. Rarely, the cool blue-grey goes cooler still, into a translucent blue-grey.

### Filaments

A layer of fine filaments covers the skin from head to foot. At rest they are long and silky and lie flat in one direction, and when a nemalor is calm they are soft and pleasant to touch. Their colour is individual, and can be any colour at all.

The filaments move. A nemalor can lift and move them at will, and they lift on their own with strong feeling. A single patch can lift without the rest.

They are part of how a nemalor senses the world. The filaments feel touch, and they feel the air moving across them, so a nemalor can feel someone coming near.

The filaments carry the species' energy, described under Magic.

### Head and face

The face is drawn forward into a fine, narrow, fox-like muzzle. The lips are thin and mobile. The teeth are sharp and pointed, and the tongue is long and narrow. Nemalor eat almost anything.

The eyes sit at a slight tilt toward the sides of the head, which gives a nemalor strong peripheral vision. The pupils are round, eye colour varies from one nemalor to the next, and they see well in dim light.

### Ears

A nemalor's ears are shaped almost like flowers. Their petals curl in different directions to change the ear's shape, which lets a nemalor focus on quiet sounds and hear across great distances. The petals can fold shut to block sound out.

The petals show feeling too, the way ears do. How many petals an ear has varies from one nemalor to the next, and the petals can be a different colour from the skin.

### Thallae

In place of hair, a nemalor's head bears a layer of thallae: thick, flexible growths that look almost like thick vines and flatten out, leaf-like, toward their tips. Their length and colour are individual.

A nemalor controls how the thallae move. They feel touch, show feeling, and can grip. How much any one nemalor moves them or puts them to use is their own.

### Hands

Nemalor hands are plantigrade. Each has six fingers, bearing no nails, and every finger is the same length, the larger thumbs on both sides of the hand included.

Each finger has several joints and can curl in either direction. A finger pad sits on both sides of every finger, and both faces of the hand are palms, so a nemalor grips with either side of the hand. Together that makes the hands highly dexterous.

### Feet

The feet are plantigrade too, with six toes and no nails. The outermost toe on each side is larger than the four between them.

Thick padding runs all over the foot, across the top as well as underneath. It is thick and firm, for walking anywhere, it grips well on slick ground, and it makes a nemalor's steps nearly silent.

### Tail

A nemalor has one long, fully prehensile tail. Filaments cover it like the rest of the body, and it can carry the energy just as they do. It can show feeling, too.

## Magic

Two things are fixed at the species level: the energy in the filaments, and the magic of the caves that grow the armour. Both are the work of [wild magic](../../magic/).

### The filaments' energy

A nemalor's filaments can give off two kinds of energy, and they use them as they choose. Both are there from birth.

The first is harmful. It can paralyse, and in some cases it is deadly painful, and nemalor use it to protect themselves. On the receiving end it comes as a sharp jolt that locks the muscles, and as a sting or burn that spreads.

The second is unerringly pleasant and soothing. It comes as warmth spreading through the body, a light tingling over the skin, or a slow, heavy calm, like sinking into rest. Depending on the nemalor's intent and feeling, it can also arouse.

The strength of both varies from one nemalor to the next, and so does how well they hold it in check. With strong feeling, either can slip out unbidden. Another being has to be close by to feel it, and it is strongest through touch. It passes through the armour as well.

It can be seen at work. When a nemalor uses either energy, the filaments glow faintly, and they lift, though not always on end.

### The caves

Deep in the homeworld lies a set of caves imbued with magic, holding underground lakes. The magic keeps anyone walking into those lakes from harm, and it is what grows the armour, as described below.

Whatever else a nemalor is tied to magically is their own.

## The armour

The armour matters deeply to nemalor, and nearly every one of them has a set, if it can be called that. It is a whole suit, which a nemalor steps into and out of.

It is grown, and it is alive. It is part of the nemalor who grew it, a living construct that grows with them and serves them in many ways.

A nemalor grows their armour only once in their life, at any age, once they feel ready. Those without armour are those who don't feel ready yet, and those who have chosen not to grow it.

### What every set shares

Every set protects its wearer. Every set grows with them, and every set heals itself when it is damaged.

The wearer feels through the armour as they would through their own skin, and it answers to their thought.

Beyond that, each set is tailored to its wearer, and no two carry the same abilities. A set might serve as a weapon and a shield, or as an apparatus for pleasure, for meditation, for communication, or for many other things besides. Depending on the set, it can let its wearer survive open space.

### Growing the armour

Every set is different, but the way it is grown is the same for every nemalor. The journey is private, and a nemalor shares nothing of their own.

A nemalor enters the caves and walks into any one of the underground lakes. They carry no food, no water and no light, nor any clothing to speak of. They walk on, deeper and deeper into the lake, and stop for nothing. The magic keeps them from any harm, even as they reach the deepest part, with the water by then likely far over their head. They find, by then, no need to breathe.

At the bottom of the lake they lie down. All this time they have been walking over a thick layer of what seem to be random segments of metal. Beneath it lies a layer of soft, layered threads, and where those threads come from is not known.

Lying there, the nemalor spreads every limb and every digit wide, and opens their mouth and eyes fully, ready to accept what the magic gives them. The magic then takes hold of their body and holds it open and ready, so that they can relax, no longer needing to hold the position themselves.

The threads rise from beneath the metal. A first layer lifts gently and runs beneath the filaments over the skin, and a second follows over the top of them. The threads then fill every opening of the body. They run around and between the fingers and toes, and enter the mouth, nose, eyes and ears, and the sexual organs too.

The armour then builds itself around the nemalor, metal and thread weaving together, over and inside every part of their body. That is why nobody but the nemalor who grew a set can enter it, except by that nemalor's express permission and action.

When the building is finished, the threads cut themselves off from the rest, finish sealing the armour, and adhere to its inside. The armour activates. The nemalor rises to their feet, wearing it, and walks back out of the cave.

The whole process is unerringly pleasant, and at times it can be pleasurable. How long it takes depends on the individual and on how complex their armour is.

### Guests

With the nemalor's permission, another person can be held inside the armour, the way its wearer is. The armour reshapes itself around them. The nemalor can withdraw that permission at any time.

### Between wearings

Where the armour rests between wearings depends on what that set can do. Wherever it is, a nemalor is never far from it, and they can feel it from a distance.

### Loss

If a set is ever destroyed beyond its own healing, it regrows from any piece that survives. Losing it hurts the nemalor who grew it.

## Intimacy and young

Every nemalor has the same sexual organs, with both male and female halves. They cannot reproduce on their own. Instead, both sets of organs are used during intimacy with another nemalor.

Pregnancy can happen to either partner, whatever their gender, because among nemalor gender is not biological. So when a couple has children, both partners usually carry one, and most such couples have at least two.

Young are born live, and are raised wherever the family lives.

## Shared instincts

Their filaments feel every touch and every stir of air, and nemalor share a comfort with touch that comes with those senses.

They share no temperament. Who a nemalor is belongs to that nemalor alone, and is recorded on their own page.

## Where the name comes from

The name is built from two roots.

**Nema** is the Greek νῆμα, *nēma*, a thread, the root inside *nematode*. It is there for the filaments that cover every nemalor, and for the threads that grow their armour.

**Lor** comes from the Latin *lorica*, body armour. It is there for the armour itself.

*Thread-armour* is the direct reading.

The **thallae** take their name from the Greek θαλλός, *thallos*, a young green shoot.
