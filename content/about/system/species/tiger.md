## Overview

Tigers are the largest of the cats, and the most individually identifiable — the striping is unique to each one, functioning much like a fingerprint.

They are considerably more social than the wild animal is. The territorial instinct is real and it is theirs, but it is an instinct rather than a rule, and a tiger can decline to act on it.

## Physical traits

Heavily muscled through the forequarters, built for short explosive ambush rather than pursuit.

The striping is unique per individual and pigmented into the skin as well as the fur — a shaved tiger is still striped. Camouflage is its purpose: tigers are orange because deer and boar are dichromatic and cannot distinguish orange from green, so against foliage a tiger reads as effectively invisible to the animals that most need to see it.

Tigers roar, and also *chuff* — a soft breathy sound used as a close-range greeting.

## Magic

The species fixes nothing. Some tigers are tied to magic heavily and some barely at all, and what form it takes is their own.

The **sacred** are the exception, and they are a breed rather than a species trait.

## Shared instincts

Tigers are territorial by instinct, and they are unusually easy in water — they cross rivers by choice and will cool off in water for hours in hot weather, which sets them apart from nearly every other cat.

Both are instincts rather than temperament, and a tiger may act against either.

Nothing about disposition follows from the species. The maternal register sometimes attributed to tigers is individual — some tigers carry it and many do not.

## Lineage

Tigers interbreed with lions, as the animals do, and with [feliry](../feliry/), whose biology is close enough to allow it.

## Where the name comes from

The tiger (*Panthera tigris*) ranges in fragmented populations across South and Southeast Asia and the Russian Far East, with the Siberian and Bengal lineages reaching 300 kg. In the wild most hunts fail and a tiger will often go a week between kills.

The reputation for aggression is largely undeserved. Wild tigers avoid humans where they can, and the animals that do not are typically injured or elderly.

## Breeds

The sacred — the white tigers — are the one tiger breed that is not simply a natural subspecies, and they are not what white tigers are in the wild.
