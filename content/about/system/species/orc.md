## Overview

Orcs come from a homeworld of their own.

They are generally on the larger and heavier side, though how heavily built any given orc is varies. Every orc has tusks, and tusk size varies as well.

Skin runs from dark earth browns through pine greens and everything in between, and it is typically not one flat colour — most orcs carry several tones at once.

There are no known orc breeds. The variation is individual.

## Magic

Every orc carries forging magic, alongside whatever else they individually have. It is an [extra force](../../magic/), and it is the one thing the species fixes.

Orcs are generally remarkable crafters and artists, and the magic is why. It flows into whatever they make, and it typically produces effects the orc themselves did not choose and cannot determine in advance. What comes out of an orc's hands tends to be more than what they set out to make.

This does not mean every orc is a maker. It means every orc has an unusually large capacity for it, and what any individual does with that capacity is their own business.

## Oaths

Promises carry real weight in orc culture. Failing to keep an oath is widely regarded as improper and dishonourable.

That is a culture rather than a temperament, and the distinction matters. It describes what orcs broadly expect of each other, not what any particular orc is like — an orc can dissent from it, fall short of it, or reject it outright, and plenty do.

The wider honour-and-kinship framework often attached to orcs in fiction is only partially real here. The part about oaths is genuine. The rest of it was not ours to begin with.

## Ageing

Orcs age much more slowly than most creatures, but they do age.

An orc can live anywhere between two thousand and ten thousand years, depending largely on how well they take care of themselves.

## Shared instincts

Orcs share the build, the tusks, the forging magic, the long life, and the cultural weight placed on keeping one's word.

They do not share a temperament. Nothing about strength, protectiveness or kinship follows from being an orc, and craft follows only as a capacity rather than as an inclination.

## Where the name comes from

Tolkien took the word from Old English *orc* — it appears in *Beowulf* in the compound *orcneas*, "demon corpses" — and connected it to Latin *Orcus*, a god of the underworld. His orcs were made in mockery of elves, bred to serve, and written as essentially irredeemable, and he was never comfortable with that; his letters return to the problem repeatedly without resolving it.

Almost everything now associated with the species postdates him. The green skin, the tusks, the clan structure and the warrior honour culture all come from tabletop and video game fantasy from the 1970s onward.

We discard Tolkien's framing entirely. An orc here is not corrupted, not made to serve, and not carrying anything that needs redeeming. From the modern convention we take the physical form and very little else; where the honour culture overlaps with ours it does so by coincidence, and only the oaths part of it is genuinely how orcs here work.
