## Name

**Meri** is the Finnish word for the sea. It also carries an echo of the Latin *merus* — pure, unmixed, undiluted — and both readings sit comfortably on her, one pointing at water and the other at something offered without conditions attached.

**Sara** is Hebrew for princess or noblewoman. Taken with the first half it reads as *the noble heart of the sea*, or more loosely as one whose openness brings warmth and belonging with it.

The sea half is the more literal of the two, given what her body does, but the pairing was chosen for the second meaning as much as the first.

## Temperament

Merisara is rarely still and almost never quiet for long. Teasing is her default register — light, affectionate, aimed at people rather than at their expense — and she is very good at judging exactly how far it can go with any particular person.

Underneath the play is something quite deliberate. She pays close attention to who is being overlooked in a group and moves toward them, usually without making it obvious that she has. Flirtation and warmth are the tools she reaches for most often, and both come from the same conviction: that people should be told, frequently and without ceremony, that they are wanted there.

She offers closeness constantly and expects nothing back for it. Being turned down does not dent her, and she does not keep score. What she does not do is push — she makes the offer plain and then leaves it entirely alone.

When someone she loves is in danger the playfulness drops away without any transition at all, and what remains underneath is flat, quiet and immovable. It is a jarring thing to see if you have only ever known the other version of her.
