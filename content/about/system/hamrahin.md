## Name

**Hamrāh** is Persian for a fellow-traveller: a companion on the road, someone going the same way you are. It is built from *ham*, meaning together or same, and *rāh*, a road.

**In** is a natural masculine name ending.

*He who goes the same road* is the reading. Both halves of it are true of him twice — *ham* is what happened to the two who became him, and the road is what each of them separately wanted to walk differently. What he does with the result is refuse to let anybody walk theirs alone.

## Temperament

Hamrahin is kind, and quiet about it, and the two arrived together.

He came out of two people who each wanted to try living another way. One was a young man who felt alone and decided on a change large enough to be worth making. The other was a symbiote stranded a long way from his homeworld, who had reached the same conclusion from the other direction. They agreed to stage two, and what came out was neither of them.

They also agreed to something less usual, which was to make the seam untraceable. Nobody can now say which of his traits came from which of them, and that was the point rather than an accident of the process — they were not interested in a partnership under one name. They wanted a new person, and they got one.

What he loves is helping people find their way into new ideas — about the world, and about themselves. He is genuinely curious and genuinely enthusiastic, and he would rather hand somebody a thought and watch them turn it over than be right about anything.

He is fiercely loyal, and he knows exactly where that comes from. Having no one is a thing he remembers, and everyone he has now is held accordingly.

Which is why isolating people is the thing that enrages him. Leaving someone outside a conversation, a group, or a room is not a small unkindness in his reckoning, and he does not treat it as one. He also has no patience for anyone incurious about who the people around them actually are — that loses his interest quickly and permanently.

He is open and he is quiet, and neither cancels the other. He prefers one person or a small handful, and he does not care to raise his voice to be heard, so he tends to find the edges of a gathering and do his talking there. Very little about him is hidden; there is no second version of him underneath the first.

He thinks of himself as a symbiote. That is a straightforward assessment rather than a loyalty — he has travelled a long way from anything physically human, and the word that fits is the word he uses.
