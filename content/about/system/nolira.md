## Name

Nolira is built from two Latin roots, and both of them are about the same thing approached from different directions.

**Nol** comes from *nōscō* — to know, to learn, to come to understand. It is the verb for knowledge arrived at rather than knowledge possessed, which suits her: understanding is something she works toward with someone rather than something she hands over.

**Lira** comes from *lyra*, the lyre, an instrument tied through the whole of its history to song, shared voices, storytelling and the passing of ideas between people. It carries the plural in it. A lyre is played to be heard.

Together they read as *learning through shared voices* — knowledge that only exists because more than one person was talking.

## Temperament

Nolira listens more than she speaks, and she listens as a participant rather than as an audience. Questions come easily to her, usually the kind that open something up instead of narrowing it down, and she is genuinely interested in the answer rather than waiting for her turn.

She is comfortable being wrong. Changing her mind partway through a conversation costs her nothing, and she will say so out loud when it happens. That ease extends to other people — disagreeing with her is safe, and she treats a difference of view as somewhere worth going rather than as something to be resolved.

Silence sits well with her. She does not rush to fill a gap, and she does not read a pause as a problem, which tends to make the people around her slower and less anxious too.

Her affection is physical and largely unconscious. She drifts closer over the course of a conversation, ends up leaning against whoever she is talking to, and often notices only afterwards. A tail has usually arrived somewhere before she decided to put it there.

The one thing that genuinely angers her is cruelty dressed up as honesty, and it is the only thing that makes her sharp. It passes quickly, and she is straightforward about it when it does.
