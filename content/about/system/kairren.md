## Name

**Kai** carries wind, open country and natural flow across several naming traditions — motion that follows the landscape rather than fighting it.

**Ren** is used in mythic naming for the watcher or the observer, and carries a sense of quiet perception and subtle connection.

*Wind-watcher of the wild* is the reading. The two halves sit oddly together on purpose: something that moves unpredictably, and something that misses nothing.

## Temperament

Kairren's cleverness is instinctive rather than constructed. Where a plan would take someone else an evening, his arrives whole and immediately, and he is usually acting on it before he could explain why it would work.

The mischief is real and it is rarely idle. His games, distractions and small deceptions almost always turn out to have been doing something — pulling attention off someone who was struggling under it, confusing a threat, or steering a situation wide of trouble before it arrived. The people he protects this way are generally the last to work out that it happened.

He watches far more than he speaks and understands considerably more than he lets on. Very little in a room escapes him, and almost none of what he notices gets mentioned.

He is never careless with his own. The humour can be sharp, and it is never aimed at anyone he has claimed.

He is equally at home in stillness and in sudden motion, with no transition worth speaking of between them. When he acts it is fast and unexpected, and it is instinct refined by a great deal of quiet observation rather than instinct alone.

## Illusion

Coyote magic runs to illusion, and that inclination is the one thing the species points at. In Kairren it sits very close to how he already thinks — misdirection is his instinct before it is ever his magic, and the two are difficult to separate in practice.

What he does with it is his own, and the trickster register says nothing about his character beyond the shape of his talents.
