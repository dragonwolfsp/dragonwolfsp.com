## Name

**Yasu** (安) is Japanese for peace, safety and tranquillity. The character itself is old and carries a domestic sense — it is the calm of a place where nothing needs guarding against, rather than the calm of a truce.

**Nari** (成) means to become, to grow, to come to completion. It describes a process reaching what it was heading toward rather than a state arrived at all at once.

Read together the name means something closer to *one through whom safety grows* than *one who is safe*. The distinction matters: the peace in her name is something she cultivates in other people over time, not a quality she simply possesses.

She shares the second element with [Kitsunari](../kitsunari/), and the parallel is deliberate.

## Temperament

Yasunari is steady in a way that is genuinely restful to be near. She does not startle, she does not escalate, and she rarely raises her voice, so people who are frightened tend to end up near her without being able to explain why.

She is affectionate through proximity and touch far more than through speech. Sitting beside someone in silence is, to her, a complete way of saying something, and she will do it for hours without any need to fill it in.

Her protectiveness is the defining thing about her, and it is careful rather than eager. She would much rather a situation never require her than get the chance to act, and she treats every capability she carries as something to be kept out of the way until there is no alternative.

Consent is something she takes seriously to the point of rigidity, particularly about herself. She is deliberate about what she opens to whom, and she does not treat closeness already given as permission for anything further.

She is warmer than her calm suggests, and it comes out most in small practical acts — noticing someone is cold, moving something heavy before being asked, remembering what a person can and cannot eat.
