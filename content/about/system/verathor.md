## Name

**Vera** comes from the Latin *verus* — true, genuine, what a thing actually is rather than how it presents. It gives English *verity* and *verify*.

**Thor** draws on Proto-Germanic roots for strength, firmness and endurance. It is worth saying plainly that this is not a reference to the deity; the root is doing ordinary work here.

Read together the name means *strength founded upon truth* — the order matters, with the conviction coming first and the durability following from it rather than the other way round.

## Temperament

Verathor is calm under pressure in a way that is genuinely difficult to disturb. He does not react quickly, and what looks like slowness is a deliberate refusal to be hurried into a choice he has not finished making.

Once he has chosen, he is immovable. He will change his mind on evidence and says so when he does, but he will not be pushed off a position by force, volume or persistence, and people generally work this out fast.

He has no appetite for intimidation and never trades on his size. Strength, as he understands it, is mostly the discipline not to use it, and he treats every occasion he does not have to as a better outcome than any he handles well.

His protection is offered by standing nearby rather than by announcing anything. People tend to feel steadier around him some time before they work out that he is the reason.

He is warmer than his composure suggests. It shows in small consistencies rather than in anything demonstrative — being where he said he would be, remembering what mattered to someone, staying past the point where the difficulty has passed.
