## Name

**Kitsune** (狐) is simply the Japanese word for fox. In his name it is doing the plain work rather than the folkloric one — he is a fox, and carries a fox's single tail, rather than being one of the [kitsune](../species/kitsune/) proper.

**Nari** (成) means to become, to grow, to achieve — a process arriving where it was headed.

The name therefore reads as *the fox who helps others become*: the growth in it points outward, at what he makes possible for other people, rather than at his own development. He shares that second element with [Yasunari](../yasunari/).

## Temperament

Kitsunari is enthusiastic in a way that is difficult to be gloomy around. He talks readily, gets visibly excited about whatever has caught his interest, and is entirely unembarrassed about any of it.

He is also constantly, quietly ready. Those two things sit together without friction — he is not braced or grim, he simply assumes something may be required of him and has already thought about what. It rarely surfaces as tension, only as the sense that he has one eye somewhere useful.

What he cares about most is other people's autonomy, and he is oddly strict about it. He would rather help someone become able to handle a thing than handle it for them, and he will step back at cost to himself to avoid taking a choice away. Being needed is not what he is after.

He is generous with trust and deliberate about it at the same time. He gives a great deal, quickly, but the things that are genuinely his to give he gives on his own terms and takes back without apology if he needs to.

Stillness does not come easily. He thinks better moving, and long stretches of nothing tend to leave him restless and looking for something to build.
