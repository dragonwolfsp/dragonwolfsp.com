Lilamori has people smiling before they have worked out why. She is bright and teasing, first to the joke and first to the wink, and her flirting unfolds like a game with the door left open, one that anyone near her is welcome to wander into and just as welcome to wander out of again. There is always a glint about her of someone who knows something you don't. Very often she does. Her teasing is warm all the way through, and being ribbed by her feels like being let in on the joke rather than made the butt of it. Underneath the play sits something steady and watchful, and it is usually noticed late, by someone who realises well after the fact that she had been looking out for them the whole time. She never mentioned it. She never will.

Her favoured form sits balanced between testrian and kitsune, an anthro hybrid in which neither heritage leads. There she stands around seven to eight feet tall, slender and lithe, with a predator's wiry strength under a dancer's grace and broad, grounded hips that carry her weight low and easy. The whole of her rests on a living spectrum, and she slides along it as easily as she breathes — taller and leaner as she leans kitsune, toward eight or nine feet, shorter and broader as she leans testrian, toward six or seven. Mood moves her along it. So does practicality. So, quite often, does a good joke, because she will happily drift a little way along the spectrum mid-conversation purely to land a point.

In her favoured form her fur and skin intermingle. Dark burnished red fur grows up through smooth, deep green skin scattered with gold, and over all of it runs a coat of slime the same deep green as her skin, carrying its gold with it, the flecks glittering in it like dust caught in a shaft of sunlight. Left to itself it is thick and warm. Where it meets her fur it slicks through without ever matting it, so that running a hand or a paw through her is a wonderful thing, soft fur and warm slime moving together under the fingers. She knows exactly how that feels. She makes the most of it. Her hands and her tails stay bare skin under slime, her head stays bare fur, and the gold carries on into the red as glints, while wherever her body leans kitsune the red darkens toward black at the paws and the tips of her tails.

### Her fox form

Her fox form is the size of an ordinary fox unless she decides otherwise, and she can grow or shrink it as far as she likes. She is sleek and long-legged under a plush, fluffy coat of dark burnished red, with a thick winter ruff at her throat and chest, ears backed in black, and a long, fine muzzle. All eighteen tails can show here as in any other form, each banded in black several times along its length, so that a full fan of them behind her is a ripple of red and black.

By default nothing testrian shows. That is a default, not a limit, because she decides exactly when and how each of her testrian traits appears. A small shell can rise between her shoulders, little more than a curl of green and gold on a fox's back, and yet it widens a long way when she wants it to, far enough that she can tuck the whole of herself inside it with ease. Fine antennae can lift among her ears. Slime can run beneath her fur, leaving the coat glossy and dark. Any of it, all of it, or none. She chooses.

### Her human form

In her human form she stands around six to six and a half feet tall, slender and lithe, with the broad hips she carries through every shape she takes. Her hair falls to the middle of her back in loose curls the same dark burnished red as her fur. Her skin is a dark golden brown and extraordinarily smooth, with gold flecks scattered across her nose and cheeks like freckles. Her mouth is wide and expressive, very ready to smile, and very ready to say something it probably shouldn't. Her eyes stay unmistakably hers.

She can pass as entirely human. She can also, whenever she likes, let fox ears rise through her curls, bring any number of tails out from the base of her spine, raise antennae from her brow, let a sheen of slime spread across her skin, unfold her shell from her back, or let her nails turn gold. Her face is hers to blend as well, and she can bring as much of her testrian or kitsune side into it as she wants, a little or a great deal, then draw it back again whenever she likes. She can do all of it at once. More often she does it one trait at a time, a tease spun out slowly in front of someone who has only just begun to suspect that the very human woman in front of them might not be.

## Name

**Līlā** comes from the Sanskrit लीला — play and sport, and also amorous play, dalliance. It is the light, unforced kind of play, done for its own sake, and in older texts it is the word for the play of the gods, who make the world simply because making it is a delight.

**Mori** comes from the Japanese 守り — guarding, protection. It is the word inside *omamori*, the small protective charm carried in a pocket or tied to a bag, which does its work quietly and without anyone needing to look at it.

Together it reads as *playful guardian*, or *the flirt who keeps watch*. The play is the part everyone sees. The guarding is the part almost no one does.

## Temperament

Lilamori flirts the way other people breathe. She winks, she teases, she leans in a little too close and grins about it, and she does it with almost everyone. None of it is pressure. The game is always open, and anyone who would rather not play only has to step back; she lets them go with the same warm grin and no hard feelings at all.

She is playful through and through. A dull moment is an invitation to her, and she will turn a chore into a contest or a serious conversation into a running joke without ever quite losing track of what actually matters. Her ribbing is affectionate, aimed to make someone laugh along rather than shrink.

Underneath it she is loyal, quietly and fiercely, and she does not talk about it. When someone she cares for needs protecting, or needs a secret kept, she does not announce anything. She simply moves something in the background: a word in the right ear, a door closed at the right moment, a problem that quietly stops being a problem. Most of the time no one realises she did it. She prefers it that way.

The two sides are not at odds. The teasing is often how she keeps watch, a way of staying close, reading the room and drawing attention exactly where she wants it, while the real work goes on out of sight.
