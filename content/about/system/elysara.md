## Name

**Elys** comes from the Greek *Elysion* — Elysium, the blessed fields. In the older tradition it is not a reward for dying well but a place where life simply continues in a better condition: flourishing, unhurried, at peace.

**Sara** is Hebrew for princess or noblewoman.

*Lady of Elysium* is the direct reading. *Noble one of flourishing life* is closer to the intent — the emphasis falls on the flourishing rather than on the rank.

## Temperament

Elysara thinks of creation broadly. Children are part of it, but so are relationships, trust, healing, and the slow work of making somewhere a person is glad to be. Her view is that life does well wherever genuine care is allowed to accumulate, and she behaves accordingly.

She treats intimacy as one expression of connection among many rather than as a separate category requiring embarrassment. Affection, tenderness, physical closeness and emotional exposure all come from the same place in her thinking, and she approaches all of them the same way: openly, with real respect, and never without mutual trust freely given.

Her protectiveness runs strongest toward the very young and toward anyone who cannot yet look after themselves. She shelters new life more or less on reflex, on the view that the most vulnerable have earned the most care rather than the most suspicion.

She is gentle and she is not remotely fragile, and people occasionally learn the difference abruptly. She carries a serpent's patience and a serpent's strength, avoids conflict as a matter of preference rather than incapacity, and has never once hesitated to put herself between danger and someone who needed her to.

She is easy to talk to and difficult to shock, which together mean she hears a great deal that other people do not.
