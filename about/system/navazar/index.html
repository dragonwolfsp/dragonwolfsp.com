Navazar

A tokren who speaks through touch, and stops the moment he is asked.

Gender
Male
Pronouns
he/him
Species
Tokren
Breed
Tokrous-Impactos

Content note. This page describes Navazar’s view of intimacy, including sexual intimacy, and a blight that can control, hurt, kill or give pleasure to those it infects. They are confined to the Temperament section’s last paragraph and to His blight, which are the parts to skip.

Name

Navāz comes from the Persian نواز, from navākhtan, to caress, to cherish, to soothe.

Zar comes from the Persian زر, gold.

The golden caress is the reading. It holds his touch, the way he shows his care, and the gold of his eyes.

Temperament

Navazar is open and honest, and he values both highly, in himself and in others. What he says is what he means.

His manner is gentle. He is often quiet, and he can be loud as well, because he follows the energy in the room and moves with it.

He protects the people around him in body and in mind alike, and he is both a dancer and a fighter.

Touch is how he shows affection, and how he helps people find their ground again. The moment anyone shows him they want him less close, or not touching them, he backs off, every time. To him intimacy is something beautiful, shared between people who trust each other, sexual or otherwise, and he will not let it be tainted.

His blight

Navazar carries a power that is his alone, a blight. It was born when wild magic twisted dark magic together, and he wields what that twisting made. He holds no dark magic of his own.

The blight spreads through his touch, his stingers, his bite and his mandibles. If he allows it, it passes onward from one infected being to the next. He can also reach out with it at a short distance, though only physical contact makes certain it takes hold. It infects only as far as he wants it to, and he chooses how damaging or how lasting it is.

In the infected it shows as dark veins and markings, and as patches of dark brown chitin. It can grow a shell of chitin that covers them completely, and that shell is different on every being it grows over.

Through the blight he can take complete control of everyone infected, from any distance, and move them as one mind. He shares as much of their senses and their thoughts as he chooses. Beings that cannot move, such as plants, become extensions of the blight if he wishes, sensory organs for the whole and sources of energy he can draw on.

He can draw energy from the infected, enough to kill if he wanted to. He can also give it to them, from himself, or pass it between them.

The infected become far stronger, faster and harder to kill than they were. The blight heightens everything they already have, physical and magical alike.

Being infected can be extremely painful or deeply pleasurable. The blight answers his wishes completely. He can remove it whenever he chooses, and it leaves nothing behind.

It can heal as well. It drives every other disease out of a living host, and it mends wounds.

It has very few limits. He can hold as many beings as he likes without any drain on himself. Only the act of infecting draws on his strength, and he is strong enough that it takes a great deal to tire him. The blight reaches beings with no physical body, such as voids and shades, because it is magical. For the same reason, magic can fight it.

Physical description

Navazar carries the presence of something old and deeply rooted, like an oak that has weathered a hundred storms and means to weather a hundred more. He is solid in a way that invites trust, the kind of steadiness a person could lean their whole weight against and feel it hold. Warmth lives in him, slow and constant, like sunlight soaked into stone that keeps giving its heat long after evening has fallen. He watches quietly, taking in everyone near him with an attention that misses very little, and that attention settles over a person the way a roof settles over a house, keeping the weather out. He is, unmistakably, a great predatory insect, built to hunt and to fight, and he wears that openly. A gentleness just as deep runs alongside it, and the two rest together in him as naturally as root and branch belong to the same tree.

He towers even among his own people, standing close to twelve feet tall, one of the tallest of the tokren. His frame is broad through the shoulders and deep through the chest, his limbs thick and corded with power beneath their plating. He is heavy and dense, carrying far more weight than even his great size suggests, and that solidity runs evenly through the whole of him, from the wide span of his shoulders down to his broad, grounded feet. When he plants himself and goes still, he looks as though he has grown where he stands.

His body is armoured in thick, glossy chitin, the plates overlapping one another like layered armour shaped by a patient hand. Each edge tucks cleanly beneath the next, sliding smoothly over its neighbour as he shifts, so that every movement flows without leaving a gap. The surface is warm to the touch, holding heat the way polished wood holds the warmth of a hearth, and those he draws close find it smooth and pleasant against them, like a sunlit stone.

His colouring is a deep, rich brown, dark as old wood steeped in rain. At his joints and along the edges of his plates it deepens further still, sinking into a brown so dark it is nearly black, tracing the lines of his armour like shadow drawn in ink. When bright light falls across him, an amber sheen moves over his plating, glowing like resin held up to the sun. In uneven light, dappled through leaves or flickering from a fire, a pattern of pale gold undertones rises through the brown: faint, shifting markings like light running beneath bark, there one moment and sinking out of sight the next.

His head is broad and heavy, strongly angular, shaped with the same solid weight as the rest of him. A heavy brow ridge shelters his eyes, and a crest runs back along the top of his head like the ridge of a hill. Prominent plates of chitin sweep along his cheeks, framing his face in layered armour. His mandibles are large and plainly powerful, strong enough to crush, their inner edges smooth. For all their strength they move with care, shifting with his expression and giving away what he feels long before he says it.

His eyes are crystal gold, very large, smooth and glassy as polished amber. They hold no visible pupils, only an unbroken depth of gold lit from within. Their glow is steady, never flickering or fading, a constant warm light like a lantern left burning in a window, and it is often the first thing anyone notices of him in the dark.

His antennae are long and thick, smooth and segmented, rising straight from his head and bending supple and flexible with every shift of his attention. They are finely sensitive to scent and to the smallest movement of the air, tasting a room as he enters it, and they read the people in it as surely as his eyes do. They are among the most expressive parts of him, dipping, lifting and turning with each change in his mood.

His hands are large and broad, each bearing eight fingers, the backs heavily plated in the same dark, glossy chitin as the rest of him. His fingertips are blunt and smooth, and the stingers within them stay hidden until he calls them out. Those great hands are built for strength, and yet they are where most of his tenderness lives, for touch is his first language, and he speaks it with every one of those fingers. Venom waits in his fingertips, his bite and his mandibles, as it does in every tokren, and his blight can travel with either venom.

He stands on jointed, insect-like legs that meet the ground plantigrade, his whole weight set down through broad, heavy feet. Each step lands with quiet certainty, as though the ground itself settles to take his weight.

From the base of his back, where a tail would begin, his body runs on into a heavy, segmented abdomen. It is plated in overlapping bands of the same dark brown chitin, each band sliding smoothly over the next, and its underside is a paler, warmer bronze. Strong muscle beneath the plating holds it firm, so it never swings or drags. He carries it low and curled slightly beneath him, a living counterweight that steadies the whole of his great frame. It moves with a slow, gentle pulse, rising and settling like breath, steady and unhurried when he is at rest and quickening whenever strong feeling stirs in him. At its tip, sheathed within the final segment, rests a stinger far larger than those in his fingertips, thick and smoothly curved. It slides out only when he wills it, and draws back out of sight when he is done. It carries both of his venoms, as his fingertips do, and his blight can travel through it as well.

His wings are beetle-like and very large, spreading wider than his arms can span. When folded, they lie flat against his back beneath hard wing covers of the same dark brown plating as the rest of him, catching the same amber sheen in the light. When those covers part and lift, the wings unfurl from beneath them, translucent and deep gold, like sunlight seen through honey. Across them runs a pattern like his blight spreading, colours creeping along the veins in many shades at once, branching and pooling as though still slowly moving.

When he dances, his wings release their glow in rolling clouds of glowing smoke, thick and slow to fade. It pours from him in heavy, drifting folds of deep gold, threaded through with every colour of his wings, and it lingers in the air long after he has moved on, so that the path of his dance hangs behind him like a trail of embers in fog.

His blight leaves no trace on him. It shows only on those he touches with it, in dark veins and markings creeping beneath their skin, and in patches of dark brown chitin the same colour as his own.

His feelings show most clearly in his antennae and his mandibles, and in his wings. His wing covers lift slightly when he is stirred, flare wide when he is roused to protect, and settle smooth and flat against his back when he is content.

He moves slowly and deliberately, each motion measured and sure, heavy and graceful at once. He carries the mantis-like flow of every tokren, and in him it takes on a deep, rolling weight, like a great tree swaying in the wind. His dancing and his fighting run into one another without a seam, each movement flowing into the next, so that it is hard to say where one ends and the other begins.

The atmosphere surrounding Navazar is that of a hearth on a cold night, with the door shut against the wind and someone large and steady sitting between the fire and the dark. Shoulders loosen near him without being told to. People who arrive frayed find their footing again, often with one of his great hands resting at their back, and the ground seems to come up a little firmer to meet them. For those inside his care, the size of him stops reading as threat and starts reading as wall, and it becomes clear that the wall has been facing outward the whole time.

He carries the scent of resin and amber, deep and golden, mixed with something warm and spiced, like bark warming in the sun beside a cooking fire. It clings softly to anyone he has held, and lingers in a room for a while after he has left it.

Navazar embodies steady shelter and gentle strength. He is a great beetle glowing in gold light, roots spreading quietly beneath the ground, and an old oak whose branches reach wide enough to shelter everyone beneath them. He is the golden caress, vast and unhurried, and wherever he reaches, he reaches gently.
