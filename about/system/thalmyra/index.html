Thalmyra

A crocodile who listens first, and moves at whatever pace healing actually takes.

Gender
Female
Pronouns
she/her
Species
Crocodile

Name

Thal comes from the Greek thalassa — the sea, or deep water generally. It carries depth rather than surface: the part you cannot see into.

Myra draws on roots associated with endurance, steadiness and lasting presence — remaining rather than arriving.

Enduring waters is the plain reading, and she who waits beneath the surface is the fuller one. Both halves describe patience rather than power, which is the right emphasis for her.

Temperament

Thalmyra listens before she does anything else, and she listens properly — all the way to the end, without preparing a response while someone is still talking. Advice comes afterwards if it is wanted, and comfort comes instead when there is no advice worth giving.

She does not rush people and does not force outcomes. Her instinct is to steady a situation rather than resolve it quickly, on the understanding that speed and healing rarely want the same thing. She will happily wait out a problem that someone else would try to solve.

She does not interfere uninvited. Being near her is never being managed, and she has an unusually clear sense of the line between offering something and pressing it on someone.

Once she has actually decided, she commits completely and there is no partial version available. She is difficult to move afterwards, and people who have leaned on her tend to describe the experience as leaning on something that was not going to shift.

There is humour in her, dry and easy to miss, and it surfaces most in the company of people who have stopped being careful around her.

Physical description

Thalmyra carries herself with a grounded steadiness that settles a space rather than filling it. She does not demand attention so much as occupy it, and the difference is obvious within a minute of meeting her. There is something ancient in the way she stands, as though rivers once shaped themselves around her and have not entirely forgotten the habit. Calm rests easily on her surface, but it is the calm of depth rather than emptiness — there is a great deal underneath it, and none of it is agitated.

She is larger than a typical crocodile, and her build is balanced and muscular in a way shaped for endurance rather than brute dominance. Strength lies layered through her shoulders and along her flanks, coiled without ever reading as tense. She is not heavy for the sake of heaviness. Everything about her proportions is constructed for long patience, sustained presence, and decisive action at the moment one is actually required, and she is entirely comfortable waiting a very long time for that moment to arrive.

Her hide is composed of smooth, river-worn scales, their surfaces refined rather than jagged, reinforced beneath by the bony plates her kind carry. Across her back and shoulders the scales hold a deep, rich red, like burnished embers beneath darkened iron. That red shifts gradually as it travels down her sides, transitioning into luminous gold across her chest and underside. The pattern is subtle and mythic, woven into the natural geometry of her scales rather than sitting on top of them. It never flashes or glares. It glows quietly, the way something old and deliberate does, and it rewards a second look far more than a first.

Her eyes resemble red sapphires — deep, multifaceted, and warm when she is at rest. In calm moments her gaze is steady and reassuring, attentive without intrusion, and there is a great deal behind it: thought, humour, and a genuine willingness to listen for as long as someone needs to talk. In the rare moments she is driven to anger, that same warmth intensifies into something fierce. The red sharpens. The fire in them becomes unmistakable, and nobody present has to be told.

Her senses run deeper than her stillness suggests. The fine dome receptors studding her jaws read the smallest disturbance in water, and her eyes, ears and nostrils sit along a single plane atop her skull, so she can lie almost entirely submerged and still hold the whole surface in her attention. It means she is nearly always aware of more than she gives any sign of noticing, and she rarely bothers to correct the impression.

Her tail is thick and powerful, yet controlled with real precision. It moves with deliberate intent and never thrashes without purpose. When she is standing near those she protects it may shift slightly, positioning itself between them and whatever she has decided merits watching — a quiet barrier rather than a dramatic gesture, and one most people never register at all.

She moves with a smooth, continuous glide whether in water or on land. Even when stationary there is a subtle sway to her posture, a barely perceptible motion that gives the impression of tide rather than stillness, and the rhythm of it can be almost hypnotic to watch. In water she becomes nearly seamless with her surroundings, flowing through rather than cutting across. When she commits to action the transition is swift and absolute, with nothing in between to warn anyone it was coming.

Strong emotion — protectiveness, resolve, restrained fury — manifests through the environment around her rather than through any outward display. Water in her presence responds. The surface begins to ripple subtly even when she stands perfectly still, as though the current itself has acknowledged her. In deeper water, faint circular distortions spread outward from where she rests, controlled but undeniable, and those who know her have learned to read the water long before they read her face.

The atmosphere surrounding Thalmyra feels like the bank of a slow river at the end of a long day, where the water has been going about its business for a few thousand years and has no intention of hurrying now. Pace drops around her. People stop finishing each other’s sentences, arguments lose their momentum without anyone conceding anything, and problems that seemed to require an immediate answer turn out to be willing to wait until morning. Her nearness offers the specific reassurance of something that has outlasted a great deal and did not find any of it especially remarkable.

She carries the scent of damp stone and river reeds, cool and grounding and immediately familiar. Beneath it runs the faint mineral edge of deep water and wet earth after rain, the smell of somewhere shaded and unhurried where the light comes through in pieces. It is not a scent that announces her. It settles into a room the way she does, and is generally noticed only once someone has already relaxed.

Thalmyra embodies depth and patience. Her loyalty is quiet and unshakeable, and she does not interfere without reason or force her will into spaces she has not been invited into. Yet once she has decided, she commits entirely — when she stands beside someone, she stands fully, and there is no partial version of it to be had. The river does not rush. But when it moves, it reshapes the land.
