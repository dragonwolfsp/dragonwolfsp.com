Leikeisa

A hellhound who keeps very still, right up until it is time to play.

Gender
Female
Pronouns
she/her
Species
Hellhound

Name

Leik comes from the Old Norse leikr: play, sport, a game. The word covered a great deal of ground, from children’s games to contests of strength to the quick back-and-forth of trading words, and it survives in the modern Scandinavian words for play.

Eisa is Old Norse for glowing embers, the live coals left when a fire has burned down and is still very much alight.

Playing embers is the direct reading, and it is close to literal for a hellhound whose fire moves along her coat. The better sense is the quiet coal that comes alive when something stirs it, and what stirs her, most often, is play.

Temperament

Leikeisa is quiet, and her quiet runs deep enough that it reads as stoic. She watches, she listens, and she is content to say very little for long stretches. People who only meet her in passing tend to come away with that stillness and nothing else.

Underneath it lives a deeply playful spirit. She loves to play with her whole body, chasing, wrestling, pouncing and being pounced on, and she loves it just as much with words. Banter is a game to her like any other, and she is quick at it, dry and well aimed, and delighted when someone answers in kind.

She loves movement for its own sake. Running, roaming, going out and doing something with the people she loves are among her favourite things in the world, and an invitation to come along is one of the warmest things she offers.

Her protectiveness is steady and constant. She keeps track of her people, and she places herself between them and trouble as a matter of course. Unjust harm is the thing that angers her most. When someone sets out to hurt another who has done nothing to deserve it, the fire in her eyes turns coal red, and all of that quiet becomes very focused.

Once someone is close to her, the warmth under the stillness is constant, and the play is never far behind it.

Physical description

Leikeisa is a great, still hellhound whose presence settles into a place as surely as bedrock, as though she has always stood exactly where she stands. Her watchfulness is quiet and complete; her tall ears may barely stir, and still very little passes near her that she has not already taken in. With those she has yet to know she is steady, calm and wholly self-contained, and once someone is close, that steadiness warms through like stone that has lain all day in the sun. Her protectiveness is quiet as well, written in where she has chosen to lie down and which way she is facing. Beneath all that stoic calm waits a deep, joyful playfulness, kept for those who come near enough to find it, and when it surfaces it arrives with the whole of her behind it.

She stands around seven feet three inches at the shoulder, a four-footed, lupine frame drawn on a scale far beyond any wolf. Her build carries the lines of a sighthound: a deep, powerful chest tapering to a narrower waist, long and lithe legs beneath, and a long neck that carries her head high above it all. The power in her chest is plain to see, and it is matched by a length of limb made for devouring distance. She is built for speed and for weight alike, as ready to run something down across open country as to plant herself in its path and stay there.

Her coat is long and rough, like a wolfhound’s, coarse along its surface and soft and dense beneath, where a hand can sink deep into it. Its colour is a dark brown so deep it is nearly black. Lighter browns and dark reds lie within it, beneath the surface, and they catch the light whenever she moves, like embers glimpsed through a layer of ash. Her mane is full and heavy and a lighter shade than the rest of her, running from behind her head down the back of her neck toward her shoulders, and the same warm, hidden shades move through it.

Hellfire lives in her coat, and she lets it roam. It is always there in some measure, playing along the patterns of her fur, following the lighter browns and the dark reds wherever they run and pouring most freely through her mane, where it moves the way wind moves through tall grass. Its colours match her eyes, running through every shade of flame from dark orange to pure white fire. It burns nothing it touches until she decides it should, so it plays harmlessly around a hand laid in her mane, a soft, living flicker against the fingers. She carries dark magic, as every demon does, and like every hellhound she cannot be burned.

Her ears are tall and fox-like, and the fur inside them fades from brown to a shockingly vibrant pink, brilliant against the darkness of the rest of her. Both ears and both tails are tipped in white. Those are the only markings she carries; the fire draws the rest.

Her head is refined and elegant, long and clean in its lines, set on a broad, wolf-like muzzle and a strong jaw that give that elegance real weight. It is a face shaped for grace and for grip in equal measure, and at rest it holds the same deep calm as the rest of her.

Her eyes are twin fires. Every shade of a flame moves through them, from dark orange through to pure white fire, and the movement never pauses, the flames shifting and curling as steadily as a hearth tended through the night. In darkness they glow, two points of living fire in the shape of a hound. When she is angry they change entirely, darkening to the heated red of a coal drawn from deep in the fire, and it is nearly always the same thing that turns them: someone attempting to cause harm that is unjust.

Those tall ears swivel fully in any direction, and she mostly keeps them still, turning them only when something catches her. They are also the first place her playfulness shows. Long before the rest of her moves, an ear flicks, tips or swings toward whoever has caught her interest, and the game has already begun.

Her two tails are long and slender, carried low when she is at rest, and each moves independently of the other, so that one may sweep lazily while its twin lies quiet. Hellfire runs along both of them to their white tips, and when they move, the light trails behind them.

Her paws are wide and heavy, as every hellhound’s are, with dark pads and dark claws. When she lets the fire reach down into them, each step leaves a faint scorch mark behind, a line of darkened prints marking where she has been.

At rest, very little of what she feels reaches the surface. When something sparks her, all of it arrives at once. The fire in her coat flares and races, her ears swing round, and her tails lift and move, the whole of her waking together in a single moment.

In stillness she is economical, spending only the motion a moment truly needs. In motion her stride is long and ground-eating, controlled and precise, and surprisingly light for something of her size, her wide paws coming down softly. She loves to run, and she runs for the joy of it, fire streaming back through her mane like a banner. When she plays, all that control loosens into something bouncy and loose-limbed, a great hound gone springy and delighted, and when the game is done it gathers itself neatly back into stillness.

Being near Leikeisa feels calm, sure and steady, like resting beside something very large that has decided it is on your side. Her stillness always holds something waiting in it, a spark ready to catch, and with it comes an open invitation to get up and go somewhere, to run, to do something together.

She smells of woodsmoke, with ash and cedar beneath it, the scent of a fire that has burned long and well through fragrant wood and settled into glowing coals.

Leikeisa is a bed of banked coals that flares bright the moment it is stirred, a great still hound lying by the hearth with one ear turned toward the door, and wildfire running free across a hillside for the sheer joy of the run. She is quiet warmth and sudden blaze in one body, and she has never once had to choose between them.
