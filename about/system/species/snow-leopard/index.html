Snow Leopard

The frost affinity carried further than anything else carries it — and impossible to freeze.

Overview

Snow leopards are built for altitude, and they carry the frost affinity further than nearly anything else does.

The cold association is not thematic. It is the actual magic of the species.

Physical traits

Everything about the build is an altitude adaptation. The chest is deep and the nasal cavity enlarged to warm thin cold air. The paws are disproportionately broad and densely furred beneath, working as snowshoes and spreading weight over loose snow. The legs are short relative to body length, lowering the centre of gravity on steep ground.

The tail is the most striking feature — nearly as long as the body, thick with stored fat, used as a counterweight during turns on narrow ledges and wrapped around the body and face for warmth at rest.

The coat is smoky grey to pale cream with dark open rosettes, and the belly fur can reach five centimetres.

Snow leopards cannot roar. The hyoid structure and vocal fold anatomy differ from the roaring cats, and the vocal range runs to chuffing, mewing and a drawn-out yowl.

Magic

Every snow leopard carries the frost affinity, and carries it far more strongly than most creatures do. Frost sits within the water group of the fundamental forces as a subset of it.

They also cannot be frozen. The immunity is absolute and it comes with the species, in the same way a tiefling cannot be burned.

Everything beyond the frost is individual.

Territory

Home ranges are large and overlap without conflict, and much of the communication between snow leopards runs through scent marking and scrapes rather than through meeting.

That does not make them solitary. Snow leopards are more social than the wild animal, and they do communicate with each other directly — though how much any of them wants to is individual rather than a species trait. That last point holds across this whole guide and is not particular to snow leopards.

Shared instincts

Composure on difficult ground is a genuine instinct: waiting, watching, and moving only when the footing and the moment are both right. So is a watchfulness that tends toward the protective — stillness that is doing something rather than stillness that is absence.

Both are instincts and neither is a temperament. A snow leopard may be nothing like composed, and how any of them carries themselves is their own.

Where the name comes from

The snow leopard (Panthera uncia) occupies the high mountain ranges of Central and South Asia — the Himalaya, Karakoram, Tian Shan, Altai — typically between three and four and a half thousand metres. It is the smallest of the Panthera cats and among the least often seen, which is where “ghost of the mountains” comes from. It ambushes from above, exploiting broken terrain, and can take prey several times its own weight.
