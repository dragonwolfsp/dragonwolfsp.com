Sideryn

Tall, scaled and two-tailed, a spacefaring people who carry the living metal of their homeworld in place of scales.

Overview

Sideryn are a tall, scaled people of open space. They walk upright on digitigrade feet, carry two fully prehensile tails, and wear plates of zoesider, the living metal of their homeworld, set in place of their scales until the plates are as much a part of them as the scales around them.

They have a homeworld, and though some visit, few live there these days. Most sideryn live out among the stars, aboard ships and in suits. They return for their plates. Every plate is set on the homeworld, and above all, sideryn return there to raise their young, because the plates have to be set and watched over from a young age. A family stays until their child’s first plate has settled.

Young are born live, usually one at a time. A sideryn lives for several centuries.

Physical traits

A grown sideryn usually stands between six and nine feet tall. Scales cover them from head to foot, broken only by the pads of the hands, feet and tails, and by their plates. Scale colour varies as widely as it does among any people; each sideryn’s own is recorded on their page.

Sideryn are cold-blooded. Their bodies make little heat of their own, and they rely on their plates and their surroundings to stay warm.

Head and face

The face is built on a humanoid base and drawn forward into a long, narrow muzzle that tapers toward the nostrils at its tip. The eyes are large and round, set beneath defined brows, above defined cheeks. The jaw is hinged to open unusually wide.

Along the jaw runs a line of small pits that sense heat. With them a sideryn can find warm bodies in total darkness, and can sense the warmth of another sideryn’s plates.

Sideryn smell through their nostrils, and their sense of smell is keen.

Their teeth are mixed: shaped for cutting, tearing and grinding, suited to eating almost anything, and sideryn are omnivores. When the mouth is closed, scaled lips cover the teeth. The tongue is long and forked, and reaches well beyond the end of the muzzle.

In place of hair, ridges run over the top of the head. They start where a human’s eyebrows would be, and where they end is individual: some stop at the back of the head, and some run on down the neck and over the shoulders. They are often a different colour from the scales, though the same colour happens too.

The ears are openings on the sides of the head, and a sideryn can seal them shut.

Expression

A sideryn’s brows and cheeks move much as a human’s do, and carry feeling the same way.

With strong feeling, the scales of the face and throat flush or shift colour. It comes on by itself, the way a blush does, and a sideryn can’t strictly control it.

Eyes

A sideryn’s pupils are round by default, and a sideryn can narrow them to slits when they choose. They also narrow on their own in bright light. Feeling can change them too, but it doesn’t have to: as with any people, not every sideryn shows a feeling the same way.

Slit pupils cut glare, so a sideryn sees more sharply in bright light, and they help a sideryn judge distance.

Each eye has a third eyelid, a clear membrane that sweeps across to clean and protect it. The two eyes move independently of each other, so a sideryn can watch two things at once.

Hands

A sideryn hand is a palm and a paw at once. The heel of the hand, nearest the wrist, is a flat palm. Out of it rises the underside of a paw, a raised central pad with smaller toe-pad mounds around it, which runs forward through the middle of the hand and then flattens back into palm around its edges. The paw sits inside a ring of palm.

Seven fingers grow from that ring: three from the front, and two from each side. The side fingers close against the front ones, so a sideryn grips from three directions at once. Every finger ends in a claw that retracts when not in use.

One thick layer of rough, squishy padding covers the whole inside of the hand, palm, paw and fingers alike, with no break anywhere. It is the same padding as on the feet, and it holds fast to almost any surface, even a smooth or sheer one.

The paw carries weight. A sideryn can drop to all fours, curl the fingers up out of the way, and run or climb on the paw pads alone. The fingers do the fine work and the paw does the heavy work.

Feet

The feet are digitigrade, with five toes, each ending in a claw that retracts like those of the hands. The pads underneath are thick, rough and protective. They are also deep and highly gripping, and hold a sideryn’s footing on almost any surface.

Tails

Behind a sideryn trail two tails. Both are extremely flexible and fully prehensile.

The last six to eight inches of each tail are wrapped all the way round in one seamless gripping pad. With them, a sideryn can grip, carry and hold as though with two extra long, powerful fingers.

The plates

A sideryn’s plates are made of zoesider, a metal found only on their homeworld. Its look varies from one vein of ore to another, and it glows faintly while it gives off heat.

Each plate takes the place of the scales where it is set. There the scales give way to metal, and the plate merges seamlessly into the scales around its edges.

The plates are alive. Run a hand over one and you will feel warm metal beneath your touch, and the sideryn will feel your hand too. The plates grow with them through their life.

Nearly every sideryn has them. A sideryn born and raised away from the homeworld may have none, and some go without because their family chose not to, or because they chose not to themselves.

Every sideryn’s plates serve as armour, and carrying them is a tradition handed down from the homeworld. Beyond that, each sideryn carries their plates for their own reasons. For some, the plates are a record of their life, told plate by plate. For some, they are a mark of family and belonging, or a living piece of the homeworld carried out among the stars.

Where the plates sit is decided first by the parents, and later by the sideryn themselves. How many they carry is decided the same way, by both, at different stages of life.

Setting a plate

The metal is warmed until it is soft, then laid against the scales and pressed in. From there it sinks in on its own, taking the place of the scales beneath it. It feels strange, though it is painless.

How long a plate takes to sink in depends on age and on the individual, anywhere from a few days to a month. Generally, the younger the sideryn, the longer it takes.

The first plate a young sideryn receives is set at a family ceremony. Every plate, first or later, is set on the homeworld, so a sideryn returns there each time they take a new one.

Setting the plates has to be watched over from a young age. A plate set badly can grow in crooked or uneven. While it sinks in, it has to be re-warmed and tended. And the plate’s magic has to settle into the young sideryn, which takes watching too. That is why sideryn, who otherwise live in space, stay on the homeworld while raising their young, until the first plate has settled.

Wear and loss

A damaged plate heals, as the rest of a sideryn does. A plate that is torn out entirely can only be replaced on the homeworld. Until then, the spot is left as bare skin, and it gives no warmth.

Embellishment

What a sideryn does with a plate is their own choice. Some leave their plates as they are. Others embellish them, and a plate can carry an image, a pattern or words.

Magic

One thing is fixed at the species level: the plates becoming a living part of the sideryn who carries them.

It is the work of wild magic living within zoesider. The magic is in the metal itself. When a plate is set into a young sideryn, it takes the place of the scales there, grows with them, and feels as living flesh would.

The same magic keeps a sideryn warm, which matters to a cold-blooded people. When a sideryn is somewhere cold, their plates give off enough heat to keep them warm, on their own, without the sideryn calling on it. That warmth can be shared, too: a sideryn can warm someone else they touch. It is not the only reason sideryn carry them, but it is part of how a scaled people lives in the cold of space.

Everything else a sideryn is tied to magically is their own.

Shared instincts

Sideryn share their homeworld, their life among the stars, the plates, the two tails, the ridges and the hands. That is the list.

They share no temperament. Who a sideryn is belongs to that sideryn alone, and is recorded on their own page.

Where the name comes from

The name carries two roots in one sound, and both are meant.

Sider comes from the Greek σίδηρος, sidēros, iron. It is there for the plates, the homeworld metal every sideryn carries in place of scales.

It also echoes the Latin sidus, sideris, a star, the root of sidereal. It is there for the stars, where most sideryn live.

The -yn ending carries no meaning of its own. It is there for the sound, as it is in mantheryn.

Star-iron is the direct reading.

Zoesider, the name of their metal, is built the same way: the Greek ζωή, zōē, life, and sidēros, iron. Life-iron, for the metal that lives.

Field notes

Individual height, scale colour, the colour and run of the ridges, and how many plates a sideryn carries, where they sit and how they are embellished, along with whatever magic they carry beyond the plates, belong on each member’s own page.
