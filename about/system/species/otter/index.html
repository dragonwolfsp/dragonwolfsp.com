Otter

The densest fur of any animal — and both the strongest will magic and the strongest defence against it.

Overview

Otters are semi-aquatic, and every species of them is built around the same problem: staying warm in water without blubber.

They are also one of the more magically distinctive species in this guide, carrying water alongside unusually strong will magic — and an unusually strong defence against the same thing.

Physical traits

Long flexible torso, short powerful limbs, webbed feet and a thick muscular tail used for propulsion and steering.

The coat is the defining adaptation and it is genuinely extraordinary — up to a million hairs per square centimetre in sea otters, the densest fur of any animal. It works as a double layer: long waterproof guard hairs over dense underfur trapping a layer of air against the skin. Unlike seals and whales, otters carry little body fat for insulation, so the coat has to be groomed constantly to keep working, and a fouled coat is a serious problem. That is body maintenance rather than anything about character.

The vibrissae are highly sensitive and can track the hydrodynamic wake a fish leaves some seconds after it has passed, allowing otters to hunt in water too dark or turbid to see through.

Magic

Two things are fixed. Every otter carries water, and every otter carries will — the fundamental affinity behind mental influence and enslavement — and the will runs very strong in them.

They also carry a very strong resistance to will magic, and to magical hypnosis, the extra force a kitsune channels as foxfire and a lamia delivers through the gaze. That combination is unusual and worth stating plainly: otters are among the hardest people here to influence against their wishes, precisely because influence is what they are built for.

Everything beyond water and will is individual.

Shared instincts

Play persists into adulthood and it is a genuine species instinct rather than a stray behaviour — it occupies real time and energy, and it sits alongside competence rather than in place of it.

Sociality is not an instinct here. In the wild it varies by species, but among otters in this guide how much company any of them wants is individually chosen.

Where the name comes from

Otters (subfamily Lutrinae) are semi-aquatic mustelids, ranging from small river otters to the giant otter of the Amazon at nearly two metres. They occupy fresh water, coastline and open sea.
