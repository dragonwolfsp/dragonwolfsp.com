Kenoris

A void who looks at the world from a little distance, and keeps every colour in his eyes.

Gender
Male
Pronouns
he/him
Species
Void

Name

Ken comes from the Greek kenos, empty: an empty space, a void.

Oris comes from the Greek Iris, the rainbow, and the messenger goddess who was its living form. The coloured ring of the eye takes its name from her. The two words run together where they meet, sharing a vowel.

The rainbow out of emptiness is the plain reading. It holds both what he is and what people remember first about him: every colour, shining out of the void.

Temperament

Kenoris is quiet and reserved. He speaks when someone speaks to him, or when he has something that matters to say, and the rest of the time he is content to listen.

He thinks slowly and on purpose. He prefers calm, philosophical reflection to a fast answer, and he lets a thought settle before he offers it.

He notices a great deal, and a dry humour runs through what he notices. It tends to surface in a small smile or a single well-placed remark.

He looks at the world from a little distance, as though standing slightly to one side of it. Others sometimes read melancholy in that; to him it is simply how he sees.

The void

Kenoris is a void: nothingness that bound itself to this reality and took on a form so that it could exist here. The form he took in his becoming is a blend of luperin and vulperin, and it holds as the becoming set it, rather than shifting between the two the way a hybrid does. Like every void, he carries what he became in full: the luperin mind waves, the vulperin claws and plating, and whatever else either people fixes.

The void he is shows through that form in his own way, in fur that drinks the light, in the shadow he never casts, and in eyes that shine with no light to fall on them. It goes no further than that.

He carries void-born magic in every reach the species knows. He can step into the void and out again anywhere he has an awareness of, other realities included; he can change parts of what he is; and he can make small alterations to reality itself. What he does with it is his own.

Physical description

Kenoris carries the stillness of a desert night — the deep, unhurried quiet that settles over open sand once the heat has gone out of the day. He is reserved by nature, speaking when someone speaks to him or when he has something worth saying, and he lets his thoughts take all the time they need, preferring calm, unhurried reflection to a quick answer. He watches the world closely and from a little distance, as though standing just to one side of it, and a dry humour runs quietly beneath all that watching. Others sometimes sense a faint melancholy in the distance he keeps; to him it is simply the way he looks at things. He seems older than he looks, and a deep gentleness sits easily on his great size.

In the becoming he took a form blended from luperin and vulperin, and the blend is fixed, set as it was in the moment he became. He stands eight feet tall, long-limbed and unhurried in every line. From the waist down he is luperin, built as a runner, with broad, heavy thighs and haunches and legs made for speed and long distance. From the waist up he is vulperin, a lean, lithe strength flowing through his long body and narrow shoulders. He holds himself upright and straight, and his height never seems to weigh on him.

The meeting of his two natures comes sharply at the waist, and it shows in his coat as clearly as in the shape of him. Anyone who knows both peoples sees the blend at once: the runner’s power below, the fox’s grace above, joined without any attempt to soften the seam.

His fur is black — a black so deep that it drinks in the light, so that looking at him feels like staring into endless darkness. Below the waist it is the short, thin coat of a desert luperin, close and fine over the long muscle of his legs. Above, it thickens into the dense, velvety coat every vulperin carries. To the touch it is simply fur, warm and soft, and yet a hand resting in it seems to sink into the dark. The black is the same over every part of him, solid and unbroken from ears to toes. His outline is sharply defined, so crisp that it seems to cut into sight itself, as though his image had been laid over the top of whatever the viewer is seeing. Wherever he stands, he casts no shadow.

Fine striping traces his head, as it does every luperin’s, and in him it carries the same prismatic shimmer as his eyes. Even in pitch darkness it shows as lit, threads of shifting colour against the black, though it casts no light on anything around it. Through it he sends and receives mind-wave messages as any luperin does.

His head is a vulperin’s, the skull a little broader than most of his kind carry, a quiet trace of his luperin side. His features are fine and sharp, drawn out into a long, narrow muzzle, and his nose and lips are as black as the rest of him, vanishing into his fur. His neck is long and lean, carrying his head with an easy, watchful poise.

His eyes are almond-shaped and startlingly bright. From a distance they look a shining white, two points of light in all that darkness. Up close they are prismatic, holding every colour of the rainbow and colours beyond it, and they never keep the same set for long: look once and see one set of colours, look away, and look back to find another. Slit pupils sit within that light, hard to make out through its brightness. Even in pitch darkness his eyes look like lights, yet they cast no light on anything around them.

His ears are tall and pointed, softly furred inside and very mobile, turning toward every sound around him. His tail is long, full and bushy, reaching nearly to the ground, and when he sits it sometimes curls around him like a drawn cloak.

His hands are where his two natures meet most closely. They are vulperin hands grown to luperin size, with long fingers and paw pads across the palms and fingertips, and the reinforcing tendons luperin carry run visibly corded across their backs. His fingertips end in retractable claws, luminous as every vulperin’s are and prismatic like his eyes, bright without ever casting light. When they extend, the layered plating of the vulperin unfolds along his forearms, and it shimmers with the same shifting colours as his eyes and claws. His feet are digitigrade, with the large, thick pads of a luperin.

His pads take on whatever light falls on them. Light of a single colour shows on them as that colour. Light made of mingled colours, such as daylight, splits apart across them into each of its colours, the way it does through a prism, so that a sunlit pad can hold a small, scattered rainbow. In darkness they turn to the same void as his fur.

The void in him shows in his light-drinking fur, his missing shadow and his shining eyes, and it stays within him; the world around him is left exactly as it is. No picture can be taken of him. A camera turned his way simply finds no one there.

His feelings show most in his ears and tail, which say far more than his face does. A small, dry smile appears when something amuses him, and his head tilts when he is curious or turning a thought slowly over.

He moves quietly, making little sound, and most of the time he is slow and deliberate. His stride is smooth and gliding, the vulperin way of walking carried on luperin legs, and each step covers a great deal of ground without seeming to hurry. When he chooses, those legs carry him very fast indeed. He has a way of simply appearing, and sometimes he literally does, stepping out of the void itself as though through a door no one else can see.

A deep quiet settles around Kenoris wherever he goes. At first glance he can be unsettling — all that dark fur, and no shadow beneath it. Once the strangeness wears off, he is calming to be near and steadying in a way that is hard to name, and to those who know him he is safe. Something faintly uncanny always stays with him, though it comes to feel less like a warning and more like part of the quiet.

He smells of dry desert air after dark, and over it lies a surprisingly strong, spice-like warmth that never stays quite the same: cinnamon one day, chocolate another, warm bread the next. It is always warm, and never twice alike.

Kenoris is a single star in an empty sky, light broken open through a prism, a desert at night and a still, dark lake. He is the rainbow out of emptiness — every colour, shining out of nothing.
