Parnegar

A gentle, playful nemalor whose living armour turns a dangerous fighter into a deadly one.

Gender
Aporagender
Pronouns
she/he/they
Species
Nemalor

Name

Par comes from the Persian پر, par, a wing or a feather.

Negar comes from the Persian نگار, negār, a painted or inscribed design. In Persian poetry the same word also means a beloved.

The inscribed wing is the plain reading. It holds the runes written across their armour and the wings that unfold from it, and the second meaning of negār suits someone so open and gentle with the people around them.

Temperament

Parnegar is playful and gentle most of the time, with a light, teasing humour that comes out once someone knows them.

She is open to new connections, curious about new people and glad to know them.

He is protective of what is his, and has no trouble standing firm, or fighting, when it is needed, which is much of why his armour is built the way it is.

They are a very steady presence, rarely unsettled by anything at all.

Parnegar is aporagender. She, he and they are all theirs, and this page moves between them as freely as they do.

Physical description

Parnegar meets the world with open arms, and people feel welcome around her from the first moment. She is curious about everyone new, eager to know who they are and what they carry with them, and she makes room for them without ever seeming to try. Seen across a room, he is striking, almost otherworldly, a towering figure with light passing through his skin, yet up close all of that softens into something warm and easy, and anyone who comes to know him soon finds a light, teasing playfulness waiting there. Beneath the play runs a steadiness that very little can shake, along with a quiet readiness to stand firm for whatever and whoever they hold as theirs.

They stand about nine feet five inches tall, slender and wiry, with the lithe build of a martial fighter. Their limbs are long, arms and legs that seem to go on and on, and most of their height rides in those long legs, rising to a narrow waist and narrow hips. Lean, corded muscle runs beneath the skin, hidden at rest and showing in every line the moment they move. A long, elegant neck carries their head high, and they stand upright and balanced without any effort at all, relaxed in it, as though standing that way is simply how they rest.

Her skin is the rare translucent blue-grey, smooth beneath her filaments and evenly translucent over the whole of her. Light passes through the thin places, the petals of her ears and the length of her fingers, and when she stands with light behind her she seems to glow faintly, with soft shadows of what lies beneath showing through. The blue-grey deepens to a darker shade at her joints, down the back of her neck and along her spine, and pales at her throat and across her palms.

Over that skin, covering him evenly from head to foot, lies a coat of deep indigo filaments. They are long and silky, lying flat in a single direction, and so smooth that from any distance they read as one unbroken sheen over his skin, a sheen that shifts toward violet as the light moves across it, so that indigo laid over translucent blue-grey makes him look like dusk settling over clear water. When he moves, the filaments ripple with him like wind passing over tall grass, and when he calls on his energy, which runs strong in him, they lift from his skin and glow faintly in that same deep indigo.

Their face is fine and delicate, drawn forward into the narrow muzzle of their kind, with high, defined cheekbones and expressive brows that carry a good deal of what they are thinking. Their thin lips curl easily into a smile, and it comes often, showing a little of their sharp teeth whenever it does.

Her eyes sit at the slight outward tilt every nemalor shares, and they are a deep, dark gold, like old amber, with flecks of dark brown scattered evenly through the colour. A striking ring of silver circles the edge of each, catching the light so that it seems to shine, and framing a gaze that is soft and steady.

His ears are large and stand tall, each one opening into a great many small petals of the same translucent blue-grey as his skin, thin enough that light passes through them, and they curl and turn to shape whatever he is listening to.

Their thallae fall to the middle of their back, thick and many, a dark forest green that brightens toward the tips, where they flatten out, leaf-like, and grow thin enough for light to show through them. They wear them loose and free most of the time.

Her hands are long and slender, with six fingers apiece whose strength is a wiry one, and they are quick and precise, always busy with something, with light passing through her fingers whenever she holds them up to it. For all their quickness, they are gentle hands, and people notice how gentle. Her feet are long and narrow, padded all over in the same translucent blue-grey as the rest of her, and they carry her almost without a sound.

His tail is longer than he is tall, slender from base to tip and covered in the same indigo filaments as the rest of him, and it is often wrapped around someone he cares about.

Their feelings show across the whole of them, and their tail moves more than anything else, swaying slowly when they are content and flicking when they feel playful. Their ear petals open wide when something catches their interest or delights them, their filaments ripple when they are amused, and their thallae curl and sway whenever a playful mood takes hold.

She moves with a dancer’s fluid grace and a martial artist’s precision, never wasting a motion, and for all her size she is nearly silent. She ducks through doorways with long-practised ease, and when it is needed she can move with a sudden, startling speed, though when she is happy there is a playful bounce in her step instead.

Being near Parnegar is like sitting beside a fire, a gentle warmth that reaches anyone close. There is a feeling of being protected around him, and of being noticed, too, with his quiet curiosity turned toward whoever is near, and a playful lightness about him that invites teasing.

They smell of cool water, like the lakes deep in the caves of their homeworld, and of green, growing things, and beneath both, faintly, of the warm metal of their armour.

Parnegar is dusk light through still water, a flower opening at night, and a lake in a cave lit from below. She is woven threads catching the light, deep forest under a violet sky, a guardian standing at a gate, and wings folded around something precious. She is the inscribed wing, gentle at rest and deadly when it matters.

Their armour

In their armour, Parnegar goes from a dangerous fighter to a deadly one. It is a living suit, grown around them in the caves of their homeworld, and it adds only a little bulk to their slender frame while making them far stronger. Dark purple and deep crimson blend into each other across its smooth, flowing plates, which follow every line of their body. A full helm closes over their head, following the long line of the muzzle and the petals of the ears, with their thallae gathered inside it, and from it rise a pair of silver horns that curve upward. Behind the helm their eyes show silver. Inside, the armour is soft and comfortable, and they can shut out every outside sensation, wholly enclosed within it.

Dark, straight claws slide from her fingertips at need and draw back again. From her back unfold a pair of bladed, mechanical wings, long and narrow and dangerously sharp. They fold away when she has no use for them, close around her as a shield, and strike with their edges, and thrusters set along them drive her through water and open space. A powerful mechanical tail encases her own and reaches further still.

Arcane runes cover the whole of the suit, dark lines that lie quiet until one of the armour’s abilities is called on, and then glow silver, like their armoured eyes. They ward against magic aimed at the wearer, hush the armour to silence for stealth, cast light in darkness, and anchor Parnegar to a surface, even in open space. Some grew with the armour in the caves and others have appeared since, and some still hold purposes they have yet to learn. What the runes mean is theirs to tell.

Hidden within the arms are two curved swords, slender and single-edged, and hidden within the back is a double-bladed axe, and at a thought the handle of any of them springs out into his waiting hand. All three carry runes like the rest of the suit. Turrets mounted on his shoulders fold away out of sight, and when he calls them up they aim wherever he looks or thinks, firing indigo, the colour of his filaments, to stun or to harm. From his chest a force field rises as a wall shimmering gold, the gold of his eyes, and he can stretch it over others as well as himself. In his palms sit plates that heat to unthinkable temperatures, glowing red-orange as they warm and white at their hottest, hot enough to fight with, to cut through metal or wreckage, or to weld, and gentle enough, turned low, to warm someone who is cold.

For all that it is made for fighting, the armour is just as well suited to rescue. It filters the air, seals itself against vacuum and stores air as it goes, so that they can work underwater or in open space, and the jets in its legs and the thrusters on its wings carry them through both.
