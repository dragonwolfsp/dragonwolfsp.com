Renuvira

A sideryn warrior and storyteller who carries everywhere she has been in silver.

Gender
Female
Pronouns
she/her
Species
Sideryn

Name

Renu comes from the Sanskrit reṇu, dust: a mote, a single grain of it.

Vira comes from the Sanskrit vīrā, a heroine, a woman warrior.

The warrior risen from dust is the plain reading. It holds the turn of her inscription, from dust to life, along with the trade she chose for that life.

Temperament

Renuvira is a storyteller, with a story for nearly everything and a gift for telling it well, though she is just as glad to sit and hear someone else’s.

She is warm and open, the kind of person who makes others welcome without ever seeming to try.

Her loyalty runs fierce and deep. Fighting is her trade, and above all she uses it to protect her crew and the people close to her.

She is quietly proud, of where she has been and of what she has made of herself, and she lets that pride show in how she carries herself rather than in anything she says.

Physical description

Renuvira carries the warmth of a lantern lit in the dark between stars, and people feel welcome around her from the first moment. She is easy to talk to and easier still to listen to, with the air of someone who has been everywhere at least once and kept a story from every place. Most of the time she is relaxed and entirely at ease, right up until something threatens her people. Her pride is real, though she carries it lightly, and it shows in the way she holds herself far more than in anything she says. She seems older and more seasoned than she looks, as though the years behind her have held more road than most, and every mile of it has left her a little steadier.

She stands eight feet tall, long-limbed and lean, with the lithe build of a warrior. Clearly defined muscle runs along her arms and shoulders, and her core and thighs are strong, made to brace and to strike. Her waist is narrow, and her power sits low, in her hips and in the heavy bases of her two tails. Years of martial training have left her posture relaxed, balanced and ready all at once, so that she stands tall and straight without ever seeming stiff, and when someone is talking to her she leans forward a little, as though drawn into whatever they are telling her.

Her scales are a deep green, glossy enough to catch the light whenever she moves. They are small and fine over her face, her throat and her hands, and larger across her back and limbs, and along the outsides of her arms and legs they rise slightly, giving those surfaces a textured feel beneath the hand. The green darkens along her spine and over the tops of her tails, and pales across her underside, from her throat down over her chest and belly to the insides of her thighs. Like any sideryn she is cool to the touch, save near her plates, where her scales hold the warmth of the metal and cool gradually with distance from it.

Her face blends fine, sharp lines with strong, angular ones. Her muzzle draws forward long and narrow to a sharply tapered tip, her cheekbones sit high and defined, and her brows are heavy and expressive, carrying a great deal of what she feels. Her smile comes readily and often, and it shows a little of her teeth whenever it does.

In place of hair, ridges of silver rise in several rows from her brow, the centre row tallest, and run back over her head and down the back of her neck to her shoulders, ending at the plate between her shoulder blades. They are firm but slightly flexible, and their silver is darker than that of her plates, a deeper, quieter metal set beside all that brightness.

Her eyes are a crystalline purple, like amethyst, darkest at the rim and lightening toward the pupil, with paler facets and flecks running through the colour like light caught inside a cut stone. They catch and hold whatever light reaches them, so that they always seem to glimmer, and the gaze behind them is steady and direct.

Her plates are bright, polished silver, the zoesider of her family’s vein. They are smooth to the touch apart from their etching, and where metal meets scale the line is clean and crisp, though never sharp or angular. When they warm her they glow faintly silver-white, and the etched lines glow more brightly than the metal around them, so that each image seems lit from within. Every plate is etched in deep-cut but very thin lines, which leaves room for extreme detail without losing any of its clarity, so that feathers, scales and fur each carry their own texture, and the style shifts a little from one plate to the next.

Along her lower jaw runs a band of small, closely set plates, one creature to each, and every one of them in motion. At the centre, on her chin, a dragon flies, and from it the others run back along either side of her jaw: a wyvern, a moon-blessed wolf running, a kitsune, a phoenix, a kraken, a gargoyle, a hellhound, a gumiho, a harpy and a plumaleon, with others besides. Toward the back of her jaw a stretch of bare scales waits for the plates still to come.

On the left side of her ribcage a plate is etched with flames, and on the right side, with a storm. Along the inside of her right bicep runs a plate carrying a single line of inscription: “From star to dust, from dust to life, from life to dust, and from dust to star again.” Between her shoulder blades, where her ridges come to an end, a pair of wings stretches toward a sun and a moon. Over her heart sits a constellation she is always adding to, and each of its stars carries a tiny image of a place she has been or a person she is close to, glowing slightly brighter than the rest of the plate. Nine talons point forward across the back of her right hand, and she carries other plates as well, though she has left her shoulders and the rest of her chest bare.

Her hands are sideryn hands, a paw set within a palm, and her fingers are built for two different kinds of work: the three at the front are long and slender, and the two on each side are strong and thick, made for closing hard against the rest, with calluses worn in where she grips her blade. Her claws are dark, close to the green of her scales, and the pads of her hands are a very dark green, like light seen through deep water. Her feet are broad and steady, with claws and pads to match her hands.

Her two tails are the same length, long enough to reach the ground and curl along it, and heavy for most of their length before tapering near the tips. The last six inches or so of each are wrapped all the way round in a seamless gripping pad, and there the green turns brighter, with a silvery sheen, so that both tails end in a gleam of light. At rest she often loops one around her leg or her waist, and when she is idle her tails tend to drift independently of each other, though when she means something by them, they move together.

Her feelings show across the whole of her. When she flushes, her face darkens to a deeper green with an undertone of red. Her tails sway slowly when she is content, and her pupils widen when she is delighted or deep in a story, then narrow again when she is angry or wary.

She moves smoothly and with balance, every step placed with care, and she is quiet on her feet despite her size. When it matters she is quick and decisive, her tails counterbalancing every turn, and when speed counts she drops to all fours to run or climb. Telling a story, she gestures widely, the whole of her caught up in the telling.

Standing near Renuvira is warm in more than one sense. There is a feeling of being looked after around her, and silences with her are comfortable ones. Beneath that easy warmth lies a steady readiness, and anyone beside her knows that nothing will reach them without going through her first.

She smells faintly of spice and smoke, the way a campfire smells long after the stories around it are over, and beneath it, of oil and warm metal from the weapons she keeps.

Renuvira is a ship’s lantern burning in the dark between stars, a storyteller beside a campfire, a shield held over someone smaller, and a map covered in the marks of everywhere she has been. She is the warrior risen from dust, carrying her stories with her out among the stars.

Her weapons

Renuvira carries a sword of zoesider named Punastara, its hilt riding across her upper back, single-edged, with a slight and wicked curve shaped for catching another blade, and a fine, sharp point cut as a triangle rather than rounded. Drawn out to its full length it reaches about four and a half feet, long even for her, and still it sits as easily in one of her hands as in both. The blade is made of linked bands of zoesider, set so close and so firmly when it is extended that it holds as strong as any solid blade, and at her will the bands wind away into the handle until only the hilt remains. It is living metal, like her plates, answering her intent and healing over any scratch or break it takes.

The blade is bright and polished to match her plates, and etched in her own deep-cut, fine-lined style: a field of stars along its length, overlaid with the flesh of many creatures, scales and fur and skin and the muscle beneath, so that the sword carries the same turn as the inscription on her arm. The etching lies quiet until she draws on the blade in a fight, and then every line of it glows, the stars and the flesh over them lit from within like the images on her plates. Its hilt is plain and practical, made for a sideryn hand, with room for her side fingers to close around it, and its wrapped grip is worn smooth where she holds it. She fights with it fast and flowing, in the martial style of her training, passing it between hand and tail in the middle of a fight, so that a stroke can come from somewhere no one was watching.

Beside the sword she carries Hoshiya, an energy rifle built far flatter than any weapon that needs a firing mechanism, light enough to hold in one hand or both and to brace against her shoulder, and it hangs from a strap across her body, at her side or her back, ready to swing up the moment she needs it. It fires a bolt of purple light, the same colour as her eyes, or a narrow, steady beam, in short bursts or as a charged shot she builds before letting it go, and it can stun as well as harm, its strength running anywhere from a light jolt to full power. It is plain, practical and well kept, and etched in her style with an image of every place it has ever been fired, so that the longer she carries it, the more of her road it holds.

At her thighs, in a sheath on each, she wears a pair of sai of living zoesider, each about the length of her forearm, two to two and a half feet. Each is a long central shaft flanked by two curved side prongs, with a sharpened tip and a grip shaped for her side fingers to close around. They are bright and polished to match her sword and her plates, and they are etched as a pair: one with the sun of every world she has been to, and the other with each world’s moon. She uses them to catch and trap a blade, as her sword is made to, as well as to strike and parry, and she will as readily hold one in the grip of a tail as in her hand, so that a fight with her can mean four weapons moving at once.

Every one of them is her own.
