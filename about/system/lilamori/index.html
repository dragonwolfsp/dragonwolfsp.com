Lilamori

A testrune who flirts in the open and keeps her guard in the background.

Gender
Female
Pronouns
she/her
Species
Testrian Kitsune
Breed
Dark-Touched
Form
Hybrid

Name

Līlā comes from the Sanskrit लीला — play and sport, and also amorous play, dalliance. It is the light, unforced kind of play, done for its own sake, and in older texts it is the word for the play of the gods, who make the world simply because making it is a delight.

Mori comes from the Japanese 守り — guarding, protection. It is the word inside omamori, the small protective charm carried in a pocket or tied to a bag, which does its work quietly and without anyone needing to look at it.

Together it reads as playful guardian, or the flirt who keeps watch. The play is the part everyone sees. The guarding is the part almost no one does.

Temperament

Lilamori flirts the way other people breathe. She winks, she teases, she leans in a little too close and grins about it, and she does it with almost everyone. None of it is pressure. The game is always open, and anyone who would rather not play only has to step back; she lets them go with the same warm grin and no hard feelings at all.

She is playful through and through. A dull moment is an invitation to her, and she will turn a chore into a contest or a serious conversation into a running joke without ever quite losing track of what actually matters. Her ribbing is affectionate, aimed to make someone laugh along rather than shrink.

Underneath it she is loyal, quietly and fiercely, and she does not talk about it. When someone she cares for needs protecting, or needs a secret kept, she does not announce anything. She simply moves something in the background: a word in the right ear, a door closed at the right moment, a problem that quietly stops being a problem. Most of the time no one realises she did it. She prefers it that way.

The two sides are not at odds. The teasing is often how she keeps watch, a way of staying close, reading the room and drawing attention exactly where she wants it, while the real work goes on out of sight.

Physical description

Lilamori has people smiling before they have worked out why. She is bright and teasing, first to the joke and first to the wink, and her flirting unfolds like a game with the door left open, one that anyone near her is welcome to wander into and just as welcome to wander out of again. There is always a glint about her of someone who knows something you don’t. Very often she does. Her teasing is warm all the way through, and being ribbed by her feels like being let in on the joke rather than made the butt of it. Underneath the play sits something steady and watchful, and it is usually noticed late, by someone who realises well after the fact that she had been looking out for them the whole time. She never mentioned it. She never will.

Her favoured form sits balanced between testrian and kitsune, an anthro hybrid in which neither heritage leads. There she stands around seven to eight feet tall, slender and lithe, with a predator’s wiry strength under a dancer’s grace and broad, grounded hips that carry her weight low and easy. None of it is fixed. The whole of her rests on a living spectrum, and she slides along it as easily as she breathes — taller and leaner as she leans kitsune, toward eight or nine feet, shorter and broader as she leans testrian, toward six or seven. Mood moves her along it. So does practicality. So, quite often, does a good joke, because she will happily drift a little way along the spectrum mid-conversation purely to land a point.

In her favoured form her fur and skin intermingle. Dark burnished red fur grows up through smooth, deep green skin scattered with gold, and over all of it runs a coat of slime the same deep green as her skin, carrying its gold with it, the flecks glittering in it like dust caught in a shaft of sunlight. Left to itself it is thick and warm. Where it meets her fur it slicks through without ever matting it, so that running a hand or a paw through her is a wonderful thing, soft fur and warm slime moving together under the fingers. She knows exactly how that feels. She makes the most of it. Her hands and her tails stay bare skin under slime, her head stays bare fur, and the gold carries on into the red as glints, while wherever her body leans kitsune the red darkens toward black at the paws and the tips of her tails.

Her face moves with the spectrum. Leaning kitsune it lengthens into a soft vulpine muzzle with fine whiskers; leaning testrian it settles rounder and smoother. Either way her features stay soft and rounded, and her face is wonderfully expressive, always caught halfway into a smile.

Her eyes are layered browns, lighter on top and deepening to almost black in darker light or on closer inspection, so that the longer anyone looks, the further down they seem to go. Long dark lashes frame them, and in the dark they hold a faint glow. The pupils move between slit and round, as every testrian’s do, with the light or with her own choosing. Every other part of her changes. Her eyes never do.

Large fox ears crown her head, tipped in a darker red and never still, turning toward whatever has caught her interest. Beside them sit short testrian antennae that twitch toward the same things and curl or droop with her mood. In her favoured form she wears both. She can let either go whenever she likes.

Her shell is a tight, elegant spiral, deep green traced with golden spirals, matte, with the texture of glazed earthenware, and when light moves across it the gold seems to turn along with it. Held close it is small and neat against her back. Folded away it is gone entirely, and the line of her back runs clean. Opened out it goes wide enough to close around herself and someone else together, and she does exactly that whenever someone wants the shelter of it — and sometimes, simply, to tease them.

All eighteen of her tails are often out at once, a great sweeping fan of them, though she shows any number she likes, from none to all eighteen. In her favoured form they are bare skin under slime, deep green flecked with gold, and wherever she lets them lean kitsune they turn to dark burnished red fur. Every one of them is prehensile. She teases with them constantly: a tickle along the back of a neck, a brush past an arm, a flick at the ear of someone who has earned it. She wraps them around the people she is protecting. And they give away what she feels before her face does, though her face is never far behind.

Her hands and feet keep the broad, muscular build and gastropod structure of a testrian, five dextrous fingers and broad soles, and in her favoured form they take on the pads and claws of a kitsune as well, the claws tinted gold. The slime answers to her. Her soles hold to a wall or a ceiling for exactly as long as she wants them to, and she climbs with it constantly, standing digitigrade or plantigrade at will, the whole of it shifting with where she sits on the spectrum.

She leaves a trail only when she means to, sometimes as a tease and sometimes as a signature. The healing her slime carries she gives quietly — a hand laid over someone’s hurt, no fuss made about it, and usually a joke to cover the moment.

What she feels shows in three places: the tails first, then her ears and antennae, then the slime, which shifts in warmth and texture as her mood moves. Between them there is very little left to guess.

Lilamori moves sinuously, swaying and unhurried, and often she glides, carried along on the slime as if the floor had been made for it. Then, without warning, she bounds. She pounces. It is all play. Given a spare sofa or a willing friend she drapes herself across it and lounges for as long as she is allowed. When she wants to be, she is completely silent.

Being near her is a teasing warmth that lets people relax. There is always a sense of mischief about to happen, and her flirtation feels safe, an invitation that anyone may turn down without it costing them a thing. Beneath all of it, people near her tend to feel quietly watched over.

She smells of warm fox musk, with undertones of honey and earth.

Lilamori is gold leaf on dark green lacquer, a wink across a crowded room, a hidden garden behind a wall, lanterns hung through a night forest, and a shell spiral catching the low sun. She is play kept for everyone. She is a guarding kept for the people she loves.

Her testrian end

At her testrian end she settles to around six or seven feet, and everything about her comes down and spreads out. She broadens through the hips and thighs until her weight sits lower still, and broadens through the shoulders and back to match, so that the dancer’s line of her favoured form gives way to something sturdier and planted. Her fur withdraws completely. Her tails go with it. Her fox ears give way to small, neat testrian ears, and her antennae lengthen, longer and far more expressive here than anywhere else, doing on their own all the talking her ears and tails usually share.

Her skin is deep green from end to end, the gold scattered evenly across her and gathered thickest over her face. It runs lighter through her chest and belly and darker along her back. Her face is soft, with a short, gentle muzzle, and still caught halfway into a smile.

Her hands are pure testrian here, broad and muscular and five-fingered, with no pads and with claws that retract away entirely, and her soles are broad gastropod soles that hold wherever she asks them to. Her slime is as it always is, there when she wants it and gone when she doesn’t, warm and thick and glittering with gold. Her shell is the same tight spiral, and this is the end of the spectrum where she most often curls into it to rest, tucked away inside the green and gold and perfectly content there.

With no tails to tease with, she teases with everything else. The antennae do a good deal of it, tipping and curling and pointing with great meaning at whoever she has decided to fluster, and the slime and her hands do the rest. She touches more at this end. A palm on a shoulder, fingers trailing down an arm, a hand that lingers just long enough to be noticed. It is not subtle. It is not meant to be.

Her kitsune end

At her kitsune end, still anthro, she draws up to eight or nine feet, and everything about her lengthens. Her legs grow long and her limbs longer, built for running and leaping. Her waist narrows above the broad hips she always carries. Her neck rises long and elegant, and the whole of her goes wiry and lean, all sinew under the fur.

That fur covers her completely, with no skin showing anywhere. It is dense and velvety all over, dark burnished red, with a thick ruff gathered at her neck and chest, and the red deepens toward black at her paws, her ears and the tips of her tails. Her shell folds away entirely. Her antennae withdraw, leaving only her fox ears. She carries no slime at all.

Her face lengthens into a full, long vulpine muzzle with fine whiskers, a black nose and black lips, still halfway into a smile. Her hands stay plantigrade and dextrous, but they draw out longer and narrower, padded now, the gold-tinted claws still there at every fingertip. Her feet rise onto her toes, digitigrade fox feet made for the running and leaping her legs were built for.

All eighteen of her tails are fully furred here, dark burnished red banded in black, and they stay that way unless she draws them back toward her testrian end. When every one of them is out at once there is a great deal of fox in the room.

Her fox form

Her fox form is the size of an ordinary fox unless she decides otherwise, and she can grow or shrink it as far as she likes. She is sleek and long-legged under a plush, fluffy coat of dark burnished red, with a thick winter ruff at her throat and chest, ears backed in black, and a long, fine muzzle. All eighteen tails can show here as in any other form, each banded in black several times along its length, so that a full fan of them behind her is a ripple of red and black.

By default nothing testrian shows. That is a default, not a limit, because she decides exactly when and how each of her testrian traits appears. A small shell can rise between her shoulders, little more than a curl of green and gold on a fox’s back, and yet it widens a long way when she wants it to, far enough that she can tuck the whole of herself inside it with ease. Fine antennae can lift among her ears. Slime can run beneath her fur, leaving the coat glossy and dark. Any of it, all of it, or none. She chooses.

Her human form

In her human form she stands around six to six and a half feet tall, slender and lithe, with the broad hips she carries through every shape she takes. Her hair falls to the middle of her back in loose curls the same dark burnished red as her fur. Her skin is a dark golden brown and extraordinarily smooth, with gold flecks scattered across her nose and cheeks like freckles. Her mouth is wide and expressive, very ready to smile, and very ready to say something it probably shouldn’t. Her eyes stay unmistakably hers.

She can pass as entirely human. She can also, whenever she likes, let fox ears rise through her curls, bring any number of tails out from the base of her spine, raise antennae from her brow, let a sheen of slime spread across her skin, unfold her shell from her back, or let her nails turn gold. Her face is hers to blend as well, and she can bring as much of her testrian or kitsune side into it as she wants, a little or a great deal, then draw it back again whenever she likes. She can do all of it at once. More often she does it one trait at a time, a tease spun out slowly in front of someone who has only just begun to suspect that the very human woman in front of them might not be.
